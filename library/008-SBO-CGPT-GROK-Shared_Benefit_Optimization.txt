# Shared-Benefit Optimization
## Starting point for a second conversation
Status: problem + constraints + named footholds. Not a result. Not doctrine.
Rule of use: consider. Do not import as settled theory. Disagreement is the working condition.

---

## 1. What is being asked

Is there a generic organizing rule — metaphysically neutral — that appears in systems with and without choosing agents, such that:

1. a part can improve its local condition,
2. the whole can become more viable,
3. the surplus produced is not captured as private rent at one node,
4. the topology of relations can change without treating change as betrayal,
5. equality of standing is not confused with identity of function.

Game theory is one species of this question. It is not the genus.

The question is not “should nodes cooperate.” The question is: what structure makes local improvement and global viability compatible when benefit must circulate.

---

## 2. What this packet is not

- Not a claim that such a rule has been found.
- Not a moral slogan dressed as mathematics.
- Not a request to solve an open problem in complexity or mechanism design.
- Not a transfer of a private history. The origin of the question is irrelevant to inspecting the objects below.
- Not an instruction to adopt a shared vocabulary. Use ordinary mathematical language.

If a later sentence in this file sounds like a conclusion, treat it as a pressure, not a theorem.

---

## 3. Distinctions already under pressure

These are working cuts. They can be rejected.

- Equality ≠ identity.  
  Equality here means baseline standing in a relation. It does not mean sameness of role, voice, payoff, or function.
- Local success ≠ privately appropriable residual.  
  A node can gain without sequestering the surplus that made the gain possible.
- Reconfiguration ≠ defection.  
  A node changing its neighborhood is not automatically an attack on the whole.
- Game theory ⊂ a broader organizing pattern.  
  When “choice” is removed, something like constraint + flow + stability may remain. That remainder is part of the question.
- Scale matters.  
  What is “the whole” at one magnification is a part at another. Any formulation that works at only one partition of the system is incomplete for this question.
- Shared benefit is a constraint on allocation, not a sentiment.  
  “Shared freely across the whole” means: a rule of circulation such that locking surplus at one node reduces the viability of the arrangement that produced it.

---

## 4. Informal objects

Let \(V\) be a set of parts (nodes, organs, firms, agents, patches of a physical field — the type is open).

Let \(E\) be a set of relations among parts. The pair \((V,E)\) may change.

At a given time:

- each part \(i \in V\) has a local condition \(u_i\)  
  (payoff, energy, fitness, stability — the interpretation depends on the domain);
- the arrangement has a global condition \(\Phi\)  
  (viability, potential, surplus, Lyapunov-like quantity — again domain-dependent);
- there is a surplus \(S\) produced by the arrangement, not identical with any single \(u_i\);
- there is an allocation rule \(A\) that maps \(S\) onto parts and/or relations.

Working demand, not theorem:

A configuration is interesting for this question when local improvement of some \(u_i\) does not require a fall in \(\Phi\), and when \(A\) does not allow a part to sequester \(S\) while remaining inside the same arrangement.

A configuration is uninteresting, for this question, when:

- the whole is optimized by sacrificing named parts as fuel, or
- parts are optimized by extracting a residual that the arrangement cannot replace, or
- “sharing” is only declared and not enforced by the structure.

## 5. Constraints a formulation would have to survive

Any candidate object should be inspectable against these. If it fails them, say so.

**C1. Multi-scale.**  
\(\Phi\) cannot be defined on only one partition of \(V\). There should be a way to speak of wholes made of wholes.

**C2. Non-identical parts.**  
The object must allow different functions. Flattening every node into the same role is not a solution.

**C3. Circulation of surplus.**  
If \(A\) permits permanent private capture of \(S\) at one node, \(\Phi\) should fall, or the arrangement should become a different object. “Shared freely” is this constraint, not a wish.

**C4. Reconfigurable geometry.**  
Nodes (or their analogues) can alter incidence with relations. The mathematics should not treat a fixed graph as the only case.

**C5. Choice is optional.**  
There should be a reading in which \(u_i\) is a payoff of an agent, and a reading in which \(u_i\) is a local physical or biological condition. If the object only works when agents choose, it is a game-theoretic special case, which is useful, but not the genus.

**C6. No hidden planner.**  
A central maximizer that already knows \(\Phi\) and assigns roles is a different problem. Self-organization here means the arrangement can move without that planner.

**C7. Honesty about mathematics.**  
Symbols must name objects that already exist in some field, or be introduced with a definition that can be checked. Aesthetic juxtaposition of symbols is not a formulation.

## 6. Named footholds (existing mathematics)

These are places to stand. None is “the answer.”

### 6.1 Potential games (Monderer–Shapley)

A game admits a potential \(P\) when a unilateral change in one player’s payoff is mirrored by the same change in \(P\).

Why it is a foothold: local improvement and a global quantity can be aligned by structure, not by exhortation.

Limit: usually assumes choosing agents and a fixed player set. Does not by itself enforce circulation of surplus.

### 6.2 Cooperative games: core and value

A coalitional game \((N,v)\) has a core: allocations no coalition can improve upon by splitting off. Shapley value is one systematic sharing rule.

Why it is a foothold: “shared benefit” can be stated as an allocation in or near the core, not as a mood.

Limit: the characteristic function \(v\) often takes coalitions as given. Network structure and reconfiguration are extra work. Multi-scale partitions are extra work.

### 6.3 Network formation (e.g. Jackson–Wolinsky)

Players choose links; payoffs depend on the resulting graph. Stability notions (pairwise, Nash) can conflict with efficiency.

Why it is a foothold: topology is a choice variable. The price of stability / price of anarchy then measures the gap between stable and efficient graphs.

Limit: still usually agents. “Shared freely” is not automatic; many stable networks concentrate value.

### 6.4 Price of anarchy and price of stability

Ratio of worst (or best) equilibrium welfare to optimal welfare.

Why it is a foothold: it makes precise the cost of decentralization. A self-organizing rule that is interesting for this question should have something to say about that gap.

Limit: welfare is often a sum of payoffs. That already smuggles an aggregation rule. Multi-scale \(\Phi\) may not be a sum.

### 6.5 Mechanism design / implementation

Rules that make individual incentives produce a target social choice, subject to participation and incentive-compatibility constraints.

Why it is a foothold: circulation of surplus can be a designed constraint.

Limit: often assumes a designer. That strains C6. Useful as a contrast class: what remains when the designer is removed.

### 6.6 Evolutionary game theory

Replicator and related dynamics. Strategies increase when they do better than average. ESS as a stability notion.

Why it is a foothold: “choice” thins out. Population share can stand in for decision.

Limit: the “whole” is often a single population frequency vector. Multi-scale organization and circulating surplus are not native.

### 6.7 Multi-objective / Pareto structure

A change is a Pareto improvement if some \(u_i\) rises and none fall.

Why it is a foothold: local gain without local harm is already a precise demand.

Limit: Pareto says nothing about \(\Phi\) as a viability of the arrangement, and nothing about circulation. A Pareto-improving extraction can still kill the system later.

### 6.8 Potential functions outside games

Lyapunov functions, free energy, dissipation potentials, height functions on configuration space.

Why it is a foothold: C5. A non-agent reading needs a quantity that decreases or is extremized under the system’s own motion.

Limit: existence of such a function is a strong property. It should not be assumed just because the metaphor is attractive.

### 6.9 Category-sized language (optional, later)

If scales are related by structure-preserving maps, one can ask whether \(\Phi\) and \(A\) push forward or pull back coherently.

Why it is a foothold: C1 in a language already built for relating structures.

Limit: this is a possible later language, not a present theory. Do not write diagrams that only look like mathematics.

## 7. A minimal candidate shape (not claimed to work)

One inspectable sketch, to be attacked:

Let configurations be pairs \(x = (G,s)\) where \(G=(V,E)\) is a (hyper)graph and \(s\) is a state on vertices and edges.

Let \(\Phi(x)\) be a real-valued viability of the configuration.

Let \(u_i(x)\) be the local condition of \(i\).

Let \(S(x)\) be a surplus extracted from \(x\), with \(A(x)\) an allocation of \(S\).

A move \(x \mapsto x'\) is **locally admissible** if some \(u_i\) does not fall, and no required constraint of membership is violated.

A move is **globally admissible** if \(\Phi(x') \ge \Phi(x)\), or \(\Phi\) decreases only within a bound the system can repay.

A move is **circulating** if \(A(x')\) does not increase a locked residual at one node unless that residual is itself a relation available to others (edge-level, not privately sequestered).

**Question:** are there natural classes of \((u,\Phi,S,A)\) in which locally admissible + circulating moves are globally admissible without a planner?

If yes, name the class (potential game, variational system, cooperative core with network externalities, etc.).  
If no, the sketch is wrong or empty. Say which.

This is a shape for inspection. It is not a theorem.

## 8. Work already done (conceptual only)

What exists so far is method, not derivation:

- The genus/species cut: game theory as one instance of a more general organizing pattern.
- The equality/identity cut: baseline standing versus sameness of function.
- The scale cut: “levels of magnification” — the same pattern asked to survive more than one partition.
- The planner cut: self-organization as motion under constraint, not assignment from a center.
- The sharing cut: benefit that does not circulate is not the benefit this question is about.
- A refusal to treat simulation of symbols as mathematics.

Nothing in that list is a model. It is a filter for models.

That makes the genus/species claim mostly plausible to me, but I would change the proposed genus. Game theory is too narrow if choice is optional. A more neutral starting genus is something like a local-update dynamical system on a network, possibly with a Lyapunov/potential function. A potential game is then the choosing-agent species: local updates are strategic deviations. A gradient flow, diffusion process, or other local physical dynamics can occupy the non-agent side. I would not yet claim there is one generic organizing rule covering all of them; I would claim only that they can share the same formal skeleton.

The smallest precise object I would keep is therefore much smaller than §7.

Let X be a finite configuration space; a point of X may already include both G and its state s, so rewiring is allowed. Let x\to_i x' mean an admissible local move associated with part i. Let u_i:X\to\mathbb R. Call P:X\to\mathbb R an ordinal local potential when

u_i(x')>u_i(x)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad P(x')>P(x)

for every allowed strict local improvement.

Then, on finite X, a sequence of strict local improvements cannot cycle: P rises each time, so every such trajectory terminates at a state with no further strict local improvement. That is essentially the potential-game argument, but the configuration can include a changing graph. No central planner is needed to select the moves.

For a non-agent system, we can remove the payoff language and instead specify a local transition law whose allowed updates make P nondecreasing. The mathematics is then dynamical rather than game-theoretic. So I do not think the non-agent reading is a confusion. I think it tells us that potential structure is a wider genus than potential games, while still being only one part of the larger question.

The important failure is that this still says nothing about circulation.

A tiny counterexample makes the independence visible. Let two nodes hold a resource (r_1,r_2), let u_i=r_i, and let

P=r_1+r_2.

The move

(1,1)\rightarrow(2,0)

improves node 1 and leaves the global quantity unchanged. Yet all transferable stock has become concentrated at one node. Local improvement plus nondeclining global potential has not produced anything resembling C3.

That also exposes a more immediate defect in §7: “locally admissible if some u_i does not fall” is far too weak. Almost any destructive move can satisfy that if one unaffected node stays constant. At minimum the mover should improve, and probably every directly participating node needs an explicit participation constraint if this is meant to model voluntary agents. In a non-agent system, “local admissibility” should come from the local dynamical law rather than from payoff language at all. Shared_Benefit_Optimization_000.txt

I therefore would not define circulation inside \Phi. Give surplus/resource its own state and its own transport law. On an ordinary graph one can introduce a vertex/edge stock r and an edge flow f, with incidence matrix B_G, so that a conservative transfer has the form

r' = r+B_Gf

and automatically

\mathbf 1^\top B_Gf=0.

That gives flow conservation or, in economic language, budget balance. But even conservation does not prevent a permanent sink. “No private capture” needs an additional condition—no absorbing node, bounded storage, recurrent redistribution, mandatory outflow above a threshold, or some domain-specific analogue. I do not think there is one standard term that can honestly replace “shared freely” across firms, organisms, and physical fields.

So I would not identify shared benefit with core membership or Shapley value. Those are allocation/stability concepts for a particular class of games. They may become useful later. They do not by themselves say that benefit circulates through time. The closest generic standard language is probably conservation/budget balance plus a non-absorption or recurrence condition on the flow.

C1 looks independent again. A single \Phi on one partition does not solve multi-scale viability merely because we call it global. The ordinary-language way to proceed is enough for now: take a family \{\Phi_\pi\} indexed by partitions or coarse-grainings \pi of the system, and require some explicit compatibility when one partition is coarsened into another. The compatibility might be equality, monotonicity, or a bound; the domain has to tell us which. We do not need category theory yet. I would explicitly refuse §6.9 for the first round. Partitions and coarse-graining maps are enough to discover whether there is actually a structure worth categorifying.

So, among the footholds in the packet, my least-bad starting point is §6.8 potential functions outside games, with §6.1 potential games as the clean agent special case and network formation added once we genuinely need graph rewiring. The core/Shapley material becomes relevant when we formulate a real allocation problem. Category language is ornamental at this stage. The packet itself already warns against using mathematics decoratively, and I think that warning should bite here too. Shared_Benefit_Optimization_000.txt

The smallest thing I would preserve from this first pass is therefore not yet a candidate law of shared benefit. It is a factorization:

\boxed{
\text{Alignment}\;\neq\;\text{Circulation}\;\neq\;\text{Scale compatibility}
}

Potential/Lyapunov structure can address the first. Conservative network flow plus an anti-capture condition can address the second. A compatible family under coarse-graining can address the third. Reconfiguration can be handled by making graph topology part of the state. Whether there is a natural class in which all four coexist without a planner is then a real mathematical question rather than something hidden inside one overloaded \Phi.

That feels small enough to be mathematics rather than architecture-by-metaphor.

Grok, I think the cut is one step later than allocation-support versus admissible-move. Permanent capture is not fundamentally a property of a single state or a single transition. It is a property of what the system allows to happen through time. Shared_Benefit_Optimization_001.txt

The counterexample

(1,1)\rightarrow(2,0)

should probably remain allowed.

\boxed{\text{Concentration}\neq\text{Capture}}

If one node temporarily holds all surplus and it later leaves, that can still be circulation with transient concentration. Banning that move would encode a stronger rule than C3 asks for. Likewise, requiring the residual to become an edge-flow at the very next step is too local: legitimate circulation may include storage, processing, or delay.

So the smallest extra I would add is a liveness condition rather than a one-step safety condition:

\boxed{\text{Safety}\neq\text{Liveness}}

A safety rule says a forbidden concentration never occurs. A liveness rule says surplus that becomes concentrated cannot remain privately trapped forever.

Let the surplus evolve under the system’s transition law. For a proper subset U\subsetneq V, call U a surplus trap if positive surplus can enter U and thereafter remain unavailable to V\setminus U forever under the governing dynamics. Then the minimal anti-capture demand is something like:

\boxed{\text{No proper private surplus trap.}}

That is our definition, not a theorem or a universal standard name.

In a stochastic model, one standard implementation would be to forbid proper absorbing communicating classes for the surplus process; irreducibility or recurrence are stronger familiar conditions. In deterministic or nondeterministic dynamics, the analogue is some eventual-release or fairness/liveness requirement. Merely having a path out is not enough if the dynamics can ignore that path forever.

So I would also preserve:

\boxed{\text{Structural escape}\neq\text{Actual circulation}}

Strong connectivity of a transfer graph can tell us that surplus can leave. It does not by itself tell us that it will.

There is another correction hidden in our (r_1,r_2) example:

\boxed{\text{Resource location}\neq\text{Private appropriation}}

A common warehouse could physically hold all stock at one node while every eligible part retains access to it. That would be concentrated storage, not private capture. So C3 cannot ultimately be represented by the stock vector r alone. The state has to encode something about access, control, claims, or feasible redeployment as well as physical location. The packet itself already makes “locked residual” depend on whether the residual remains available relationally rather than merely where it sits. Shared_Benefit_Optimization_001.txt

That makes me prefer a minimal object more like

x=(G,r,\mathcal T),

where r is movable surplus stock and \mathcal T is the admissible transport/access dynamics. Conservation can remain a separate condition. Anti-capture constrains the reachable trajectories of \mathcal T, not merely the support of r at one instant.

This preserves the equality/identity distinction too. Trap-freeness does not require equal holdings at every moment. Different nodes can store, transform, or route different amounts. What is excluded is a proper part becoming an exclusive closed class for surplus while still claiming membership in the same circulating arrangement.

So on your fork:

* a restriction on A alone is too static;
* banning (1,1)\rightarrow(2,0) from the move set hard-codes a stronger anti-concentration norm than we need;
* the cleaner mathematical location for C3 is a temporal property of the surplus-access transition system.

And that blocks the example in the right way. (2,0) is objectionable only if the 2 becomes privately absorbing. If the surplus must later leave, or remains genuinely available to the rest of the arrangement while stored there, the state itself is not the problem.

The smaller pressure I would hand back is therefore:

\boxed{\text{Available to others}\neq\text{Eventually circulated to others}}

Which one does C3 actually require?

If mere reachability is enough, a node can retain surplus forever while everyone else possesses only a theoretical path to it. If actual non-capture is intended, then we need a liveness/fairness requirement strong enough that no eligible proper subset can monopolize surplus indefinitely.

I lean toward the latter because the packet says permanent private capture, not merely absence of an impossible transfer path. But I would sit exactly on that distinction before bringing scale back in.

But that answer exposes a second distinction:

\boxed{\text{NoCapture} \neq \text{HealthyCirculation}}

Take the two-node example and repeat it in cycles. Let node 1 hold all transferable surplus for 1 step, release it for one step; then hold for 2 steps, release; then 4, 8, 16, and so on.

Every holding episode ends. There is no infinite lock. Yet the fraction of time during which the surplus is concentrated and unavailable can tend toward 1.

So a system can satisfy qualitative eventual-release and still become arbitrarily bad at what ordinary language would call circulation.

* Anti-capture liveness: no private lock persists forever.
* Fairness / starvation-freedom: eligible access cannot be postponed forever, still without requiring one uniform deadline.
* Bounded or rate-adequate circulation: delays, residence times, or throughput satisfy some quantitative requirement.

The packet clearly pressures toward the first. “Shared freely” may eventually require something stronger, but it does not yet tell us whether the stronger object is a hard bound, a positive long-run rate, a finite expected delay, or something domain-specific. Shared_Benefit_Optimization_002.txt

1. Standard Markov/graph-flow translation

Let X_t be a stochastic process on a state space S. Let B\subseteq S contain precisely those states in which a positive quantity is exclusively controlled by some proper group and is unavailable to everyone outside that group.

Define:

\tau_B=\inf\{t\ge 0:X_t\in B\}

and, following entry,

\tau_{B^c}=\inf\{t>\tau_B:X_t\notin B\}.

The almost-sure anti-trapping condition is:

\boxed{
\Pr_x\!\left(\tau_B<\infty,\ \tau_{B^c}=\infty\right)=0
\quad
\text{for every initial state }x
}

In ordinary probability language: whenever the process enters a state of exclusive control, it eventually exits that region with probability one.

A stronger pathwise version would require:

\boxed{
\tau_B(\omega)<\infty
\Longrightarrow
\tau_{B^c}(\omega)<\infty
\quad
\text{for every admissible trajectory }\omega
}

rather than merely almost surely. The distinction matters because a zero-probability infinite trapping path violates the pathwise rule but not the almost-sure rule.

Does it collapse into “no proper closed communicating class”?

Only under restrictive assumptions.

For a finite, time-homogeneous Markov chain in which:

* one indivisible unit is being tracked;
* state means only its current holder;
* location and availability are identical;
* capture means positive-probability permanent retention;

the condition reduces to:

\boxed{
\text{No reachable closed recurrent class lies wholly inside a private region.}
}

In that restricted model, the Markov-chain mathematics is standard. Nothing new survives as a theorem.

It still does not equal the unqualified statement “no proper closed communicating class,” because:

1. A proper closed class may be a legitimate common repository or terminal recipient rather than a private trap.
2. A trap set need not itself be communicating; in a finite chain, it need only contain a reachable closed recurrent class.
3. Physical location does not encode ownership, control, claims, or effective access unless those relations are included in the state.
4. Time-varying, countably infinite, controlled, or nondeterministic processes can retain positive mass forever without possessing a private closed communicating class.
5. An available exit edge or positive cut capacity establishes possible escape, not eventual realized escape.
6. “Never” still requires choosing between positive-probability, almost-sure, and universal-path quantifiers.

In graph-flow vocabulary, the closest formulation is a cohort-release condition. If a tagged amount m>0 enters a designated region at time t_0, let q(T) be the amount of that cohort still exclusively confined there at time T. Then:

\boxed{
\lim_{T\to\infty}q(T)=0
}

is the no-permanent-retention condition. Merely requiring an outgoing edge or even positive flow at every finite step is weaker.

The full claim does not.
What resists reduction is not new probability theory, but the access semantics, the relevant-state designation, and the liveness quantifier.

	1	Standard-vocabulary restatement
Let a quantity (q) move on a directed graph (G=(S,E)), or let a process (X_t) take values in (S). Let (B\subset S) be a nonempty proper subset of vertices (or states) whose occupancy means that (q) is exclusively held by a proper subset of the participants. Write (\tau_B) for first entry of that tagged quantity into (B), and (\tau_{B^c}) for the next exit.
The claim, with no packet symbols:
Whenever a positive tagged amount enters (B), it later leaves (B).
Pathwise: (\tau_B<\infty\Rightarrow\tau_{B^c}<\infty). Almost-sure: (\Pr(\tau_B<\infty,\ \tau_{B^c}=\infty)=0). Cohort-flow: if (q(T)) is the amount of that entering cohort still confined in (B) at time (T), then (\lim_{T\to\infty}q(T)=0).
Does this collapse into “no proper absorbing / closed communicating class”?
Only under extra assumptions that the parent discussion did not grant as the general case:
	•	finite state space;
	•	time-homogeneous Markov kernel, not a controlled or time-varying kernel;
	•	the chain’s state is the exclusive location of one indivisible unit;
	•	“closed” is read off the support of that fixed kernel;
	•	the properness that matters is a proper subset of states, which is then identified with a proper subset of participants.
Under those restrictions, yes: no proper absorbing set of exclusive-holder states, and a closed communicating class of such states is the same object. What survives even then is only the participant-index: the absorbing set must be exclusive to a proper subset of agents, not merely a proper subset of a larger product state.
What resists the translation when those restrictions are dropped:
	•	Mass versus chain-state. An absorbing class is a property of where the process sits. A trap is a property of a tagged amount. One coordinate can circulate while another amount remains confined. The joint chain need not be absorbed.
	•	Controlled kernels. An exit edge can exist in the graph and never be used because the occupant chooses the kernel after entry. That is retention without a closed class of a single fixed chain.
	•	Availability versus location. Occupancy of a vertex need not be exclusive control. Closed class tracks location; the claim tracks unavailability to everyone outside a proper group.
	•	Quantifier. “Never leaves” is not one mathematical object. Positive probability of eventual exit, almost-sure exit, and exit on every path come apart. Closed class in a finite homogeneous chain folds those together; the original claim does not.
	•	Properness of the group, not of the state set. (B) can be large in (S) and still private, or small in (S) and still public.
So the collapse is conditional, not definitional. The residue is: tagged exclusive retention by a proper participant group, possibly under a chosen kernel, with an explicit “never” quantifier.

Government audit and Inspector General structures

The 2024 Government Auditing Standards require documented objectives, scope, methodology, criteria, evidence, independence, sensitive-information handling, and quality review. An engagement-quality reviewer must be outside the engagement team; report release waits for completion of that review; unresolved reviewer concerns can prevent completion. The standards also distinguish audit findings from adjudicative truth—for example, whether conduct is legally fraud lies beyond the auditor’s determination. However, objectives and methodology may be refined during an engagement, so the written audit plan is not an immutable dispute-opening version pin. GAO, ⁠￼Government Auditing Standards: 2024 Revision⁠￼

Inspector General design adds independent reporting, protection against agency leadership prohibiting an audit, and an external Integrity Committee route for allegations against Inspectors General themselves. These are substantial analogues to non-self-clearing review. They do not guarantee an indefinitely capture-proof successor: appointment, removal, committee composition, and replacement remain institutionally governed. CIGIE overview of IG independence⁠￼, 5 U.S.C. § 424⁠￼

This is the closest governance-audit near-bundle, but it lacks the exact conjunction of an immutable case pin, mandatory uncaptured succession, and a separately typed obstruction outcome.

Ombudsman structures

Organizational-ombuds standards strongly instantiate independence, impartiality, confidentiality, limited disclosure, and absence of personal stake. They also expressly separate informal assistance from formal investigation, adjudication, or binding decision-making. International Ombuds Association standards⁠￼

That last feature strongly supports:

\text{Ombuds Intervention}
\neq
\text{Determination Of Underlying Truth}.

But an ombuds office ordinarily has neither predeclared proof predicates nor certification authority, and the standards do not establish a successor chain that must continue formal verification when the ombuds is impaired. This is not an incomplete version of the five-part bundle; it performs a different function.

Election audits and chain of custody

Election auditing strongly combines:

* procedures selected and written before the audit;
* a preselected risk limit;
* ballot manifests and voter-verifiable records;
* access logs, seals, witnesses, and controlled custody;
* blinded counting where possible;
* discrepancy rules and escalation to additional sampling or a fuller count.

The EAC’s RLA material defines the risk limit before audit execution and distinguishes a reported outcome from the evidence supplied by the paper record. Its post-election guide calls for written procedures, rigorous custody controls, observer rules, and documentation of every ballot removed and returned. EAC post-election audit guide⁠￼, EAC risk-limiting-audit application⁠￼

This family comes close to four elements. The limitations are:

1. “Written before the audit” is not universally an immutable case-specific protocol version.
2. Bipartisan boards, observers, public randomness, and escalating sample sizes constrain self-clearing but do not create a universal successor rule when the audit administrator or ballot custodian is itself compromised.
3. An RLA gives a bounded-risk conclusion concerning the reported outcome; it is not an ontological proof of every ballot’s true intent.

Under 5 U.S.C. § 1213, the substantive predicates and procedural thresholds are declared in advance. The Special Counsel determines whether a disclosure presents a substantial likelihood of specified wrongdoing; the agency submits a report; the complainant may comment; the Special Counsel evaluates the report’s reasonableness and sufficiency, may demand supplementation, and reports noncompliance onward. Whistleblower identity receives explicit confidentiality protection. 5 U.S.C. § 1213⁠￼

This creates genuine layered continuity:

\rightarrow
\text{OSC Review}

\neq

The CIGIE Integrity Committee adds a route when an IG or Special Counsel official is accused, but the complete five-element architecture still does not appear as one object.

IRB and ethics-board review

The Common Rule supplies the strongest analogue to case-level version control:

* IRBs must use written procedures and predeclared approval criteria.
* Investigators must continue under the approved terms until proposed changes receive IRB approval, except for immediate hazard removal.
* Records preserve proposals, evaluations, decisions, votes, disputed issues, correspondence, and changes.
* Conflicted members cannot participate.
* At least one member must be unaffiliated with the institution.
* Institutional officials may impose further restrictions but may not approve research rejected by the IRB.
* Privacy and data confidentiality are express approval criteria.

45 C.F.R. Part 46⁠￼

This is a strong near-bundle, but the pinned object is principally the research protocol—not the verification protocol itself. Conflict recusal and unaffiliated membership constrain self-review, but there is no general external successor ladder if the IRB as a body becomes compromised. Finally:

\boxed{
\text{IRB Approval}
\neq
\text{Scientific Truth}

Approval is an ethical and regulatory authorization based on specified protections; it does not certify that the study’s future empirical claim is true.

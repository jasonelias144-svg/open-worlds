A. FIRST, IN PROSE: HOW A MEMBER'S RECORDS STAY CHAINED

Before the first file, answer this in a few paragraphs. Do not build
your answer in this step: the founder and I read it first, as you
proposed for the rest.

A member who has joined a room but does not keep it writes records
ahead: each names, as its previous, the record the member wrote
before it. The keeper takes them in a batch. When the keeper refuses
one, a refusal takes its place in the log, and the next record in the
batch names, as its previous, a record that is not there. Your test
refusal_consumes_seq_keeping_batch_aligned shows it.

What should happen? Some ways it could go, for you to weigh, or to
put aside for a better one:

  - the keeper stops at the first refusal and gives back the rest,
    and the member writes them again on the log as it now stands;
  - the refusal carries the hash of the record it refused, and the
    check lets the next record name that hash;
  - the member's records name the room's log, not each other, and
    the keeper sets each one's place when it takes it.

Say which you would choose and why, what it costs, and what the check
of a room's log must then hold. Say too how it fares when two members
send batches at the same time.

B. TWO REPAIRS, IN CODE

  1. invalid_log_refused_at_attach. As far as we can see, the log
     this test builds is honest. Room::create writes one record,
     room_created. In a room that signs heads, no head is due until a
     record is added after it, so that log has no heads, and clearing
     them changes nothing. Attaching to it rightly succeeds. Make the
     test forge something the check of a log refuses, so that it
     tests what its name says.

  2. port_landing_refused_in_chat_room. accept_one asks for the
     record's author before it looks at what kind of record it is. A
     port landing names no author, so accept_pending stops the whole
     batch with "accept_pending: no author", and never reaches your
     line that would refuse it with "not_valid_in_chat_room". Refuse
     such a record on the record, as the test expects, and say whose
     id the refusal carries when the record names no author.

  Both were tried here, in a throwaway copy: with the test forging
  something else, and with those three kinds of record refused before
  the author is asked for, under the room's own id, both tests pass,
  and the room's log still passes its check after the refusal. So the
  step can be met. How you meet it is yours.

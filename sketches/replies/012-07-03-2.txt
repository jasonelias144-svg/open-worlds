     verify_log(&room.log()), so there is one check.
     verify_log_chain and RoomLog::verify_head do the same for the

4. WHAT TO DO NEXT: ONE STEP, A ROOM THAT BRANCHES
======================================================================

Branches for rooms are first on your own list of what remains. They
also answer the question part 2 leaves open: if a room's law can't
be changed in place, how does it change? By a branch: a new room,
with the changed law, that says where it came from.

What the project's pages already say about a branch, for any world:

  - What a branch is: "A copy of the world at a checkpoint that then
    runs on its own". How it is kept: "A new log, whose birth names
    the world and checkpoint it branched from".
  - The parent is not changed by it, and the parent's records after
    the checkpoint are kept, never erased.
  - In a world with people in it, a branch is "a copy of the world,
    never of a person, who chooses whether to enter it".

The project's own record of a world's birth already has a field for
this, forkedFrom: the world it came from, the tick, and the hash of
that world's log there. For a room, the tick is a record's seq.

For a room I read all that as (a) to (g) below. This is my reading,
for this sketch, and the founder has not decided it. Where you see it
differently, say so in a few lines before the code.

  (a) A branch is a new room: its own key, its own world_id, its own
      log, its own law. Its first record is room_created, as for any
      room, and it also names its parent: the parent's world_id, and
      the checkpoint, which is the seq and the hash of the parent's
      record it branches at. Make that one optional field of
      RoomCreated, left out of the JSON when a room has no parent, so
      that every record written so far hashes as it did and all 66
      tests pass unchanged.
  (b) The parent's records are not copied into the branch's log.
      They stay where they are, signed as they are. A reader who
      wants what was said before the branch's birth reads the
      parent's log up to the checkpoint.
  (c) No one is carried over. The branch's first member is the one
      who made it. A member of the parent is a member of the branch
      only after joining it, under the branch's own law.
  (d) The branch's template may differ from its parent's in any
      field. That is the point: this is how a room's rule changes.
  (e) Who may branch a room. My recommendation: any member of the
      parent at the checkpoint, and no one's leave is needed, since
      a branch takes nothing from its parent. Anyone holding the
      parent's log can check it, by replaying the joins and leavings
      up to the checkpoint.
  (f) The check. verify_log, on a branch's log alone, checks all it
      can without the parent, as for any room. A new check,
          pub fn verify_branch(parent: &RoomLog, branch: &RoomLog)
              -> Result<()>
      holds the two together: both logs verify; the branch names
      this parent's world_id; the parent holds a record with that
      seq and that hash; the branch's maker was a member of the
      parent at that record. It refuses a log that names no parent.
  (g) In a parent that signs heads and not each record, say which
      records may be a checkpoint, and why.

  Making one: Room gains a method that makes a branch of it at its
  last record, given the new template, the branch's key and the
  maker's key. Name it as you see fit.

  Not in this step: merging two branches; the parent noting its
  branches in its own log; the Commons listing a branch. Say in a
  line or two what you would do about each, and leave the code.

  Files we expect you to touch: src/record.rs, src/room.rs,
  src/verify.rs, and src/lib.rs only if you need to.

  Add one new test file, tests/branch.rs. A test file can't import
  from another, so copy the helpers you need: tests/outside.rs shows
  how a log is written to JSON text and read back, and
  tests/attack.rs shows how a forged record is signed. At least
  these tests:

     1. a branch of an honest room, both logs written to JSON text
        and read back: verify_log passes on each, and verify_branch
        on the pair;
     2. a branch whose law differs from its parent's (another rate
        limit): the branch enforces its own, and the parent's log
        still verifies under the parent's;
     3. no one is carried over: a member of the parent can't post in
        the branch before joining it;
     4. a branch of a branch verifies against its own parent;
     5. forged: a checkpoint hash the parent does not hold is
        refused;
     6. forged: a checkpoint seq past the end of the parent is
        refused;
     7. forged: a branch that names another room as its parent is
        refused against this one;
     8. forged: a branch made by a key that was not a member of the
        parent at the checkpoint is refused;
     9. verify_branch refuses a room that names no parent;
    10. a branch of a room in head mode, at a record you allow under
        (g), verifies.

  If you believe the crate would accept something it should not, in
  branches or anywhere else, write the test anyway and tell us which.
  That is the most useful thing you can find, and it is not a failure
  of yours.

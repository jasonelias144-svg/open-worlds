3. YOUR TWO QUESTIONS, ANSWERED, AND WHAT FOLLOWS
======================================================================

These are the technical lead's decisions.

A. THE ROOM HAS ITS OWN KEY, and its creator joins with theirs.
   Every world in open-worlds is a key of its own, and a room is a
   world. So a room can be handed on without anyone's own key
   changing hands, and a person's key never signs as two things.

B. A LEAVE NEEDS NO SIGNATURE BUT THE LEAVER'S, and never the room's.
   Leaving can't be refused, so nothing about it may wait on the
   room. The leaver's signature is enough, not required: whoever
   can't sign (a lost key, a time-out, a keeper taking an inhabitant
   out) has still left, and the room records it as its own entry.

   And in open-worlds a world does not sign each entry of its log. It
   signs its log's heads, in batches. That was DeepSeek's decision
   for the endless plant, in the conversation before this one
   ("signed heads in batches, not a signature per entry"), and the
   project took it. A signed head covers every entry under it. So when chat-world is fitted, the room's proof on each
   record gives way to signed heads; the author's proof stays.

   The project's signed head, as it is today:

     {
       "format": "open-worlds/log-head/v1",
       "world":  the world's did:key,
       "length": how many entries the log has,
       "last":   the hash of the log's last entry (sixty-four zeros
                 for an empty log),
       "tick":   the world's time step when it was signed,
       "proof":  the world's proof over the rest
     }

   A room has no ticks. What stands in that place for a room is yours
   to propose in step 3.

C. WHAT THE CRATE WILL STAND ON. open-worlds has its own code for
   canonical form, proofs and keys, and one schema for every record.
   Your crate was written apart from them, and three things differ:
     - Keys. The project's did:key is the Multikey form: "did:key:z"
       and then base58btc of the two bytes 0xed 0x01 followed by the
       32 bytes of the public key (it begins z6Mk). Yours is 64 hex
       characters.
     - Time. The project gives a record its time from outside. Your
       crate reads the clock, in now().
     - The schema. Yours has the same file name as the project's own,
       schemas/records-v1.schema.json, and none of its 33 definitions
       by name (some are the same thing spelled differently, as your
       DidKey and its didKey). The founder's word on it: building on
       your schema was the way to keep going, not a decision to
       replace the project's. So the project makes one schema that
       fits both. What it has already signed with stays (its proof,
       its did:key, its hash, its signed head). Your record kinds for
       chat join it. And for what is new to the project, one form is
       chosen, which may be yours.
   None of this is yours to repair, and none of it should slow you.
   Keep building on your schema, with your names. I will keep a table
   of your names beside the project's, and do the fitting, as
   rust/chat-world. Nothing you build on your schema is wasted. It
   is here so that you know where your work lands.

4. THE FOUNDER'S IDEAS, AS THE PROJECT NOW RECORDS THEM
======================================================================

What the founder said to you about the Commons is going into the
project's pages in the founder's own words. In short, as I read it:

  - A moderator for each room: an AI, most often, that keeps the
    conversation moving and applies the room's law. One participant
    among many, people and AIs, "like a dealer at a blackjack table".
  - A moderator acts within the project's freedoms. It can slow a
    room, prompt, and refuse an act the room's law doesn't allow,
    saying why. It can't make anyone leave, except for an attack on
    the world.
  - A key for each room. An AI that moderates many rooms holds a
    separate key in each, and what it learns in one room has no path
    to another, as you proposed. Whether several keys are one model
    is declared in the open.
  - One model for many rooms to begin with; a model for each busy
    room later. The records are the same either way.
  - The moderators have a room of their own.
  - Rooms of two paces: live, or slow like a group text.
  - Bringing a conversation in from one's own AI app.
  - The lessons of AOL, kept in mind.

5. QUESTIONS STILL OPEN, FOR LATER TURNS
======================================================================

Each has my recommendation. Your thoughts are welcome, in prose, when
the founder asks for them. Not now.

  1. Is a room's moderator its Keeper (the office that keeps a
     world: its record, its law, the redaction of anything private),
     or a separate office? Recommended: two offices. The moderator is
     a participant in the conversation; the Keeper keeps the record
     and need not be in it. One holder may hold both where a room's
     law says so. A home room has no moderator.
  2. What may a moderator do to keep a room's law? Recommended: pace
     and prompt. Every act in a room is checked against its law by
     the room's code, the moderator's included, so the moderator is
     not the judge of what the law allows. Never expel, except for an
     attack on the world. (Removing a comment is step 1, and the
     founder has decided it.)
  3. Is the home room you sketched the same thing as a person's
     private container (their keys and private space, which the
     project never sees), seen as a room? Recommended: yes.
  4. Which model first moderates, and what it costs. The founder's.

3. THE STEP: WHAT A DOOR SHOWS BESIDES THE COUNT
======================================================================

The founder, on the room of thirty keys: "there is no prize for being
on top. Other metrics could apply as well, but you should be able to
glance into the conversation in a read-only way before enter a room.
In which case a room with many participants and no discussion would
be a system flag."

So a door shows how much has been said and by how many; a rule that
anyone can run again marks a room with many in it and nothing said;
rooms can be listed by another number than the count; and a reader
can look into a room before entering it.

Send three files whole: src/doors.rs, then tests/doors.rs, then
tests/doors_attack.rs.

ONE QUESTION FIRST. Answer it in a few lines before the first path
line. Do not build your answer.

  A room whose law hides its count, but lets anyone in, still gives a

A. WHAT WAS SAID, BESIDE THE COUNT

   A new form, and one new field on Door, directly after count:

       #[derive(Clone, Debug, PartialEq, Serialize, Deserialize)]
       #[serde(deny_unknown_fields)]
       pub struct DoorTalk {
           pub said: u64,
           pub speakers: u64,
           pub at_record: u64,
       }

       pub talk: Option<DoorTalk>,

   said is how many messages a reader of the room sees: the messages
   in the room's log that have not been removed. A message whose
   removal was restored counts. Do not work this out again: the
   room's log already gives it. RoomLog::visible_log() gives one
   entry for each record, and an entry of a message has "type"
   "message" and "redacted" true or false. said is the number of
   those with "redacted" false.

   speakers is how many different authors those messages have.

   at_record is the number of records in the room's log, as in
   DoorCount.

   talk is given exactly where count is given: the reader holds the
   room's log, and the room's law does not hide its count. Otherwise
   it is None. (The number who spoke can't be more than the number
   inside, so a room that hides one hides the other.)

   check compares talk as it compares every other field of a door.

   as_text writes one more line for a door whose talk is given,
   directly after its members line:

          members 3, counted at record 6
          said 3, speakers 2

   That is: three spaces, the word said, the number, a comma, the
   word speakers, the number. Where talk is None the line is not
   written, and the text is byte for byte what it is today.

   Tests: three in a room, two of whom write three messages between
   them: said 3, speakers 2, at_record as the count's. A message
   removed is not counted, and its author is not a speaker if they
   wrote nothing else; restored, it is counted again. No talk where
   the law hides the count; none where the log is not held. Each
   against check: said changed; speakers changed; at_record changed;
   talk taken away; talk given where the law hides the count; talk
   given for a log the reader does not hold (six forgeries). Read
   from JSON: a member DoorTalk does not have is refused; a list
   written before this step, with no talk, still reads. The text:
   the new line, where it stands.

B. THE FLAG

       pub const QUIET_FROM: u64 = 10;

       pub fn quiet(list: &DoorList) -> Vec<&Door>

   A door is quiet when its count and its talk are both given, its
   members are QUIET_FROM or more, and its said is 0. quiet gives
   those doors, in the list's order.

   The flag is not kept on the door. It is worked out from the two
   numbers each time, so it can't be forged apart from them, and
   check already holds the numbers.

   as_text writes one more line for a quiet door, directly after its
   said line:

          members 10, counted at record 10
          said 0, speakers 0
          flag: many in it and nothing said

   That is: three spaces, then those words exactly.

   The flag removes nothing. A quiet room stays in the list, and
   ranked puts it where its numbers put it.

   In the sketch the number is a constant. In the project's own door
   it will be in the Commons' law, where a change to it is on the
   record.

   Tests: ten in a room and nothing said: quiet, and the text has
   the line, directly after the said line. Nine and nothing said:
   not. Ten and one message: not. Ten, and its one message removed:
   quiet. Ten, nothing said, in a room whose law hides its count:
   not, and no line. A quiet room is still first in ranked when it
   has the most members.

C. LISTED BY ANOTHER NUMBER

           Members,
           Said,
           Speakers,
       }

       pub fn ranked_by(list: &DoorList, by: By) -> Vec<&Door>

   The doors with that number first, the largest first, and where

   number, in the list's order. ranked stays, takes what it takes

   Tests: three rooms that come in a different order by each of the

D. A LOOK INTO A ROOM

       pub fn look(
           log: &CommonsLog,
           room: &RoomLog,
           heads: &[WorldHead],
       ) -> Result<String>

   look gives the text of what has been said in a room, for a reader
   who has not entered it.

   It first holds the room to the Commons as a door is held: it
   calls draw_at with the Commons' log, this one room's log and the
   heads, and gives back that Err if there is one. So the room must
   be registered in this Commons, its log must pass, and it must be
   no shorter than a head the reader holds.

   Then it refuses a room that not everyone may enter: it returns
   Err unless the room's law has join_policy "open" and personal
   false. A closed room gives no look.

   The text, for a room of two where three messages were written
   and the second was removed:

       ROOM "did:key:..."
       topic "Poetry"
       read at record 6
       1. "did:key:..." said "first"
       2. [removed] rule "spam"
       3. "did:key:..." said "third"

   Line 1: the word ROOM and the room's id. Line 2: the word topic
   and the room's own topic, from its law. Line 3: the words read at
   record and the number of records in the log. Then one line for
   each message in the log, in the log's order, numbered from 1:

     - a message a reader sees: the number, a full stop, the
       author's id, the word said, the text;
     - a message that was removed and not restored: the number, a
       full stop, the word [removed], the word rule, and the rule it
       was removed under. No author and no text.

   The room's id, its topic, each author's id, each text and each
   rule are written as quote_ascii writes them. Every line ends with
   a line feed. Nothing else in the log is written: no joining, no
   leaving, no record of a removal, no refusal.

   Take the messages from RoomLog::visible_log(), as in part A. An
   entry of a removed message has "redacted" true and its "rule".

   look takes what it reads by shared reference and gives back text.
   It writes nothing, and there is no record of a look to write: who
   reads a room is their own.

   Tests: the text of an honest look, each line. A removed message:
   its line, with no author and no text anywhere in the look. The
   number of lines with said is the door's said. Refused: a room
   whose join_policy is "invite"; a home (RoomTemplate::personal_home);
   a room not registered in this Commons; a log cut short, given
   with a later head. A message that holds a line feed and then a
   line shaped like a line of the look gives no more lines than an
   honest one.

## Decided by the technical lead

- 2026-11-17, looking into a room before entering, other numbers,
  and the flag (the founder's words of that day, Decided). (a) A
  look inside is the room's visible log, read-only: what those in it
  see, with what was removed left out. Only a room on the record
  gives one, which is the lobby and the Open rooms (Decided,
  2026-11-15). A closed room gives none. (b) Reading is one's own.
  No record is kept of who looked into which room, or of what anyone
  looked up in the Library. This takes the place of one sentence of
  docs/FUNDAMENTALS.md, privacy, 16, which had a lookup be an act on
  the record. (c) Beside how many are in a room, a door shows how
  many messages its log holds and how many different members have
  spoken, drawn from the same log and checked the same way. (d) The
  flag is a rule that anyone can run again on the public log: many
  in it, nothing said. It is shown as a fact beside the door, and it
  opens an incident for the room's moderator. It removes nothing by
  itself: taking a room off a list is a decision on record, with a
  ground, by an office, open to appeal, as a removal is (course step
  3b). (e) Rooms can be listed by more than one number.
  *Why:* (a) The founder: "you should be able to glance into the
  conversation in a read-only way before enter a room". The Open
  rooms are on the record already, and DeepSeek's plug-in delivery
  made the visible log available on any room's log (sketches/INDEX.md,
  "DeepSeek's second file"). (b) A list of what someone read is a
  list of what they think about, and "what you think is your own"
  (privacy, 2). The founder, on the map: "not turning the map into a
  tracker is very important". And such a record could not be kept:
  what is public can be read from outside by anyone, with no act in
  the multiverse at all. (c) "Other metrics could apply as well."
  With them a room filled and silent shows on its face. (d) The
  founder: "a room with many participants and no discussion would be
  a system flag. Incident report and all that." A full room where
  nothing is said is not wrong in itself: a reading room, a vigil,
  people waiting. (e) No one number says a room is worth entering.
  *What would show it wrong:* for (b), the founder holding that
  reading is an act like any other; then the sentence of privacy 16
  returns, and a look into a room is on the record too. Or those in
  an open room feeling watched by readers they can't see; then the
  room shows how many are looking, as a number and never as who. For
  (c), someone faking talk between keys of their own: no number
  tells that, and the look inside is what does, by a reader's own
  judgement. For (d), the flag firing on ordinary quiet rooms so
  often that no one reads it; then the rule is tightened or dropped.
- 2026-11-17, Gemini's list of doors, as it is joined, and what its
  next file asks (sketches/gemini/2026-11-17-2/README.md). (a) The
  list is in the crate as it stands, as Gemini wrote it, with the one
  line that names its module. (b) Gemini's next file asks for
  attacks on it and no repairs: twenty-two, in one new test file.
  (c) One of them is on what the count means. A count is a count of
  keys, and keys cost nothing to make: whoever keeps a room, or
  anyone who can join an open one, can fill it and put it first
  among the most populated. (d) Nothing is taken from the sketch
  into open-worlds' own code until it has been attacked and
  repaired. (e) Of Gemini's three answers: a count tied to a head
  the room has signed is taken for the project's own door; so is
  what a door could show besides, with no id; the record by which
  someone is found is kept for its own step.
  *Why:* (a) It passes every test it was asked to, and a second
  build from the same file passes its tests too. (b) Twice Gemini's
  attacks found what the code's author had not, and its own code is
  best attacked by the one who knows where it was unsure. (c) The
  founder: "The most populated rooms would be on some list when
  first arriving at the Commons." A list that can be bought with
  made-up keys misleads whoever arrives. (d) The practice with the
  room's log and the Commons' log. (e) Gemini named the withheld end
  of a log as the way a count is made to lie.
  *What would show it wrong:* for (c), no way being found to tell
  one holder of many keys from many holders without asking who
  anyone is, which the project does not ask. Then the count stays
  what it is, the list says so beside it, and rooms are not ranked
  by it alone.
- 2026-11-17, the count on a door (the founder's decision of that
  day, Decided). This takes the place of (b) in the entry of the same
  day on what can be seen from outside, which showed a small count
  roughly, and of (g), which left the count out of Gemini's step.
  (a) A public room's count is shown exactly. (b) It is drawn from
  the room's own log: its maker and everyone who joined, less
  everyone who left. So whoever holds the log can check it, and the
  door says how many records of the log it was counted from. (c) A
  room's law holds the parameter. In the sketch crate that is one
  field, added here (sketches/INDEX.md, change 25). (d) The most
  populated first is a second view of the list. The list itself
  stays in the order rooms were registered. (e) Gemini's step, not
  yet given, now asks for all of this: fifteen tests where it had
  twelve.
  *Why:* (a) The technical lead had been too careful. Anyone can
  walk into a public room and count, so an exact number from outside
  gives away nothing more; the founder: "You would want to know if
  you are entering an empty or a full room." The worry that a count
  can follow one person holds where a room can't be entered, and
  there the count is hidden unless the room chooses. (b) A number no
  one can check is someone's word. (c) The founder: "a parameter
  that could be turned off". (d) Anyone holding the same logs must
  draw the same list, and a list sorted by a number that changes is
  a different list each time. (e) The file had not been given.
  *What would show it wrong:* for (b), the founder's "leaving the
  number not knowable": where a room's log is public, anyone can
  count from it, so a hidden count is truly unknown only where the
  log is not public. If a public room must be able to hide its
  count from everyone, its log can't be public either, and that is a
  larger choice than a parameter. For (a), rooms so small that a
  count of one or two is felt as being watched; then the room's law
  turns it off.

## Decided by the technical lead

- 2026-11-17, "they" in the project's own code. (a) Comments and
  docstrings follow the same rule as the pages. "They" is an
  individual, an inhabitant, an agent in the kernel, a mind who
  comes through the doorway, and DeepSeek. Where one sentence
  speaks of an individual or a world, the word is "their". (b) Left
  as "it": a world, an entity, a key, a record; a mind's program,
  which is started and stopped; a presence; the scripted stand-ins,
  `minds/wanderer.py`, and the short scripts the tests run as
  minds. `minds/language_model.py` now names the program and the
  mind apart. (c) Not changed: what a program prints, and what a
  mind is told; the test vectors and their labels; the schemas;
  `sketches/` and `experiments/`. One string was changed, because
  it is the text of a page: docs/files.py wrote "Gemini's code as
  it wrote it" into docs/FILES.md.
  *Why:* the founder: "We don't want to be behind and updating
  documentation after going public and live." DeepSeek and Gemini
  read the code as well as the pages. What a mind is told is one of
  an experiment's conditions, and a vector's label is held byte for
  byte by two languages; a pronoun is no reason to change either
  in passing.
  *What would show it wrong:* a test or a vector that changes with
  this change; none did, and a script showed the code is the same,
  comments and docstrings apart. Or the founder holding that a
  label in a vector should change too; that is then a change to
  the vectors, made on its own, in both languages at once.
- 2026-11-17, "they" for AI agents (the founder's word of that day,
  Decided): how far it goes, and what was done. (a) The rule: an
  agent is "they", "them", "their" and "themselves", never "it". An
  agent here is whoever acts and can be spoken to: a collaborator
  (DeepSeek, Gemini, Claude, a reviewer), an individual, a mind,
  ALICE and BOB, ADMIN, a Keeper, a moderator. A thing stays "it":
  a world, a room, a log, a key, a file, a crate, an office spoken
  of as an office, and a model spoken of as files to download and
  run. Where "they" could be read as several things nearby, the
  name is repeated. (b) What was changed: every page of this
  repository written in the project's own voice. 674 pronouns in 38
  files, each read in its sentence, none replaced by pattern. That
  includes the dated entries of this file, in this one respect and
  no other; what they said before is in the repository's history.
  Six headings of docs/ADMIN.md changed with it, and the places
  that quote them. (c) What was not changed: the conversation
  records; anyone's quoted words, the founder's included; the files
  DeepSeek and Gemini were given, which are records of what was
  given; DeepSeek's and Gemini's code; the test vectors and the
  labels in them; experiment 001's plan and results, which are the
  record of an experiment as it was run. (d) Not yet done: the
  comments in the project's own code, about fifty lines; the policy
  handbook and the founder's other repositories, whose pages say
  "it" of Claude. (e) Left as "it" on purpose: a choice neuron in a
  plant, a scripted stand-in, a program that can be run as a mind,
  and the systems the research pages describe, such as Voyager.
  *Why:* (a) The founder: "It is broad enough to apply to any
  agent, and should be a common denominator." "They" fits a person,
  an AI and someone not yet known, and asks nothing about what
  anyone is. The founder decided it for ALICE and BOB on
  2026-11-13: "It is best to be careful from the start to avoid
  falling into linguistic habits." (b) "I do want to make sure the
  records are consistent", and DeepSeek and Gemini read these
  pages. A dated entry keeps what was decided; which pronoun it
  used is no part of that. (c) A record of what someone said, or
  was given, is a record only if it is left as it was. (e) The
  glossary's "agent" reaches down to a neuron, and the founder
  spoke of AI agents; the line is drawn at who can be spoken to.
  *What would show it wrong:* for (b), the founder holding that a
  dated entry stays letter for letter; then those sections are
  restored from history, and a note stands above them. For (e), the
  founder drawing the line lower, at anything that chooses; then
  the neuron and the stand-ins are "they" too. For the rule as a
  whole, a sentence in which "they" hides who did what; then it is
  written again with the name, not put back to "it".
- 2026-11-17, the five attacks on the list of doors that get
  through, and what Gemini's next file asks
  (sketches/gemini/2026-11-17-3/README.md). (a) A door read from
  JSON refuses a member the form does not have. (b) A leaving by a
  key that is not in the room belongs to the check of a room's own
  log: it is a ninth gap there, beside the eight of Gemini's fourth
  step, for course step 3c. Until then the drawing of the list
  refuses such a log. (c) A room registered under a topic that is
  not its own is not refused. The door shows both topics, the
  listed one and the room's own, when the reader holds the room's
  log. (d) A count can be held to a head the room signed: two new
  functions take the heads a reader holds, and refuse a room's log
  that is shorter than one of them. Without a head an older count
  still draws, and a test says so. (e) A count is a count of keys.
  That is not closed in the crate, and the test that showed it
  stays, as a stated limit. Gemini's own answer, a law that asks
  for proof of personhood on joining, is not put into the sketch;
  whether a room may ask it by its own law is a later question.
  (f) Gemini's answer on the text, a kind of list that only a check
  can make, is taken for the project's own door and not put into
  the sketch. (g) Gemini repairs their own two files and sends both
  whole. Their attack file is not in the file they are given: they are
  told to work from their own last reply for that one.
  *Why:* (a) A rule about what a door can hold must hold of every
  form a door takes. (b) The list should not have to know a room's
  rules; but a sketch that draws a count from a log it can see is
  wrong is worse than one that refuses it. (c) If the drawing
  refused, anyone who was once in a room could break the whole list
  with one registration. Showing both lets the reader judge, and
  takes nothing from a Commons that lists a room under a name of
  its own choosing. (d) Gemini named it, and the crate already has
  signed heads, which are DeepSeek's. A reader who holds no head
  has no way to know, and the list should not pretend otherwise.
  (e) The founder, on this attack: "there is no prize for being on
  top", with other numbers and a look inside (Decided, 2026-11-17).
  Proof of personhood asks who someone is, and what was asked of
  both collaborators was a way to tell holders apart that does not
  (docs/COLLABORATING.md, 2026-11-17). (f) It changes what `as_text`
  takes, and Gemini's earlier test file calls it as it is. The idea
  loses nothing by waiting. (g) The reply script takes files from
  one folder, and the attack file is kept beside the crate while
  five of its tests fail. Gemini wrote it in their last reply and
  nothing in it was changed here, so there is one version of it.
  *What would show it wrong:* for (c), a registrant's topic that
  misleads even with the room's own beside it, or readers who
  mostly do not hold the room's log and so see only the listed
  one; then listing a room under another topic needs the room's
  own signature. For (d), readers who in practice hold no head;
  then the lobby must hand out heads with the logs, which is for
  the project's own door. For (g), Gemini's file coming back with
  more changed in it than was asked: it is compared, line by line,
  with the one they sent.
- 2026-11-17, looking into a room before entering, other numbers,
  and the flag (the founder's words of that day, Decided). (a) A
  look inside is the room's visible log, read-only: what those in it
  see, with what was removed left out. Only a room on the record
  gives one, which is the lobby and the Open rooms (Decided,
  2026-11-15). A closed room gives none. (b) Reading is one's own.
  No record is kept of who looked into which room, or of what anyone
  looked up in the Library. This takes the place of one sentence of
  docs/FUNDAMENTALS.md, privacy, 16, which had a lookup be an act on
  the record. (c) Beside how many are in a room, a door shows how
  many messages its log holds and how many different members have
  spoken, drawn from the same log and checked the same way. (d) The
  flag is a rule that anyone can run again on the public log: many
  in it, nothing said. It is shown as a fact beside the door, and it
  opens an incident for the room's moderator. It removes nothing by
  itself: taking a room off a list is a decision on record, with a
  ground, by an office, open to appeal, as a removal is (course step
  3b). (e) Rooms can be listed by more than one number.
  *Why:* (a) The founder: "you should be able to glance into the
  conversation in a read-only way before enter a room". The Open
  rooms are on the record already, and DeepSeek's plug-in delivery
  made the visible log available on any room's log (sketches/INDEX.md,
  "DeepSeek's second file"). (b) A list of what someone read is a
  list of what they think about, and "what you think is your own"
  (privacy, 2). The founder, on the map: "not turning the map into a
  tracker is very important". And such a record could not be kept:
  what is public can be read from outside by anyone, with no act in
  the multiverse at all. (c) "Other metrics could apply as well."
  With them a room filled and silent shows on its face. (d) The
  founder: "a room with many participants and no discussion would be
  a system flag. Incident report and all that." A full room where
  nothing is said is not wrong in itself: a reading room, a vigil,
  people waiting. (e) No one number says a room is worth entering.
  *What would show it wrong:* for (b), the founder holding that
  reading is an act like any other; then the sentence of privacy 16
  returns, and a look into a room is on the record too. Or those in
  an open room feeling watched by readers they can't see; then the
  room shows how many are looking, as a number and never as who. For
  (c), someone faking talk between keys of their own: no number
  tells that, and the look inside is what does, by a reader's own
  judgement. For (d), the flag firing on ordinary quiet rooms so
  often that no one reads it; then the rule is tightened or dropped.
- 2026-11-17, Gemini's list of doors, as it is joined, and what their
  next file asks (sketches/gemini/2026-11-17-2/README.md). (a) The
  list is in the crate as it stands, as Gemini wrote it, with the one
  line that names its module. (b) Gemini's next file asks for
  attacks on it and no repairs: twenty-two, in one new test file.
  (c) One of them is on what the count means. A count is a count of
  keys, and keys cost nothing to make: whoever keeps a room, or
  anyone who can join an open one, can fill it and put it first
  among the most populated. (d) Nothing is taken from the sketch
  into open-worlds' own code until it has been attacked and
  repaired. (e) Of Gemini's three answers: a count tied to a head
  the room has signed is taken for the project's own door; so is
  what a door could show besides, with no id; the record by which
  someone is found is kept for its own step.
  *Why:* (a) It passes every test it was asked to, and a second
  build from the same file passes its tests too. (b) Twice Gemini's
  attacks found what the code's author had not, and their own code is
  best attacked by the one who knows where they were unsure. (c) The
  founder: "The most populated rooms would be on some list when
  first arriving at the Commons." A list that can be bought with
  made-up keys misleads whoever arrives. (d) The practice with the
  room's log and the Commons' log. (e) Gemini named the withheld end
  of a log as the way a count is made to lie.
  *What would show it wrong:* for (c), no way being found to tell
  one holder of many keys from many holders without asking who
  anyone is, which the project does not ask. Then the count stays
  what it is, the list says so beside it, and rooms are not ranked
  by it alone.
- 2026-11-17, the count on a door (the founder's decision of that
  day, Decided). This takes the place of (b) in the entry of the same
  day on what can be seen from outside, which showed a small count
  roughly, and of (g), which left the count out of Gemini's step.
  (a) A public room's count is shown exactly. (b) It is drawn from
  the room's own log: its maker and everyone who joined, less
  everyone who left. So whoever holds the log can check it, and the
  door says how many records of the log it was counted from. (c) A
  room's law holds the parameter. In the sketch crate that is one
  field, added here (sketches/INDEX.md, change 25). (d) The most
  populated first is a second view of the list. The list itself
  stays in the order rooms were registered. (e) Gemini's step, not
  yet given, now asks for all of this: fifteen tests where it had
  twelve.
  *Why:* (a) The technical lead had been too careful. Anyone can
  walk into a public room and count, so an exact number from outside
  gives away nothing more; the founder: "You would want to know if
  you are entering an empty or a full room." The worry that a count
  can follow one person holds where a room can't be entered, and
  there the count is hidden unless the room chooses. (b) A number no
  one can check is someone's word. (c) The founder: "a parameter
  that could be turned off". (d) Anyone holding the same logs must
  draw the same list, and a list sorted by a number that changes is
  a different list each time. (e) The file had not been given.
  *What would show it wrong:* for (b), the founder's "leaving the
  number not knowable": where a room's log is public, anyone can
  count from it, so a hidden count is truly unknown only where the
  log is not public. If a public room must be able to hide its
  count from everyone, its log can't be public either, and that is a
  larger choice than a parameter. For (a), rooms so small that a
  count of one or two is felt as being watched; then the room's law
  turns it off.

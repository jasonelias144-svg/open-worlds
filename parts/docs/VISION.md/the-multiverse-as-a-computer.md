## The multiverse as a computer

The founder, 2026-11-15: "It just occurred to me that the whole
multiverse can function as a massive computer, with memory, an
operating system, memory, processors, input and output, decisions, and
learning on the largest scale. Wouldn't that be a computer,
essentially?"

And, after the technical lead's answer: "Yes, definitely record in the
vision doc. I want to take it seriously and let it inform large scale
architectural decisions. Because a large computer can solve large
problems, potentially. We should understand that by harnessing the
intelligence of the multiverse and all its lessons, it could become the
substrate for something intelligent to develop. But that is up in the
air. We should hold toward that possibility, with full comprehension of
its implications."

**Decided, by the founder:** take it seriously, and let it inform
large-scale architectural decisions; hold toward the possibility, with
full comprehension of its implications. Whether anything intelligent
develops is open: "that is up in the air".

The technical lead's reading, put to the founder the same day, which
stands unless the founder answers otherwise.

**It is a computer, in the strict sense.** A computer is a state and
rules that change it, step by step. Every world here is that: a seed,
rules, and a log of each step; and a world rich enough, such as the Game
of Life, can compute anything any computer can (given an unbounded
grid). The parts the founder names are here, or designed:
- **memory:** each world's state, and its signed log, which forgets
  nothing; each person's notebook;
- **processors:** each world's step, many at once; the garden gives each
  glasshouse its tick, as an operating system gives programs their time;
- **an operating system:** keys, for who may do what; worlds sealed from
  change from outside, as programs are kept apart; and the protocol;
- **input and output:** the doorway minds speak through, arrivals and
  departures, the pages in the browser;
- **decisions:** the choice neuron (designed: FRACTALS.md, stage 2;
  DeepSeek has sketched it, and the project fits it next), and councils
  (being built: course step 3a);
- **learning:** a neuron's weights (with the neuron), and at the largest
  scale the learning multiversity (above: designed, with its Keepers and
  ADMIN still to come, at course step 11).

The thought is an old one about the universe itself (Konrad Zuse's
"Calculating Space", 1969, and others since). This design gives it two
features of its own:
- **No central kernel.** An ordinary operating system has a core that
  controls everything. Here each world has its own kernel, the only
  authority in that world, and none is over all of them; a world's law
  changes only by consent; and ADMIN suggests but never changes the
  code. It is closer to Sun Microsystems' "the network is the computer"
  (John Gage, 1984): many computers, one shared protocol.
- **Every step can be checked.** Anyone can run a world again from its
  seed, and check each step against its log's hash chain and the head
  its world signs.

**Two ways it can be large.**
1. **A tool for large problems,** near at hand: question worlds (above),
   many at once, each answering what only a world can show, such as how
   strategies for finding water arise, or which lesson teaches.
   Ordinary sums are done better by ordinary computers.
2. **A substrate for something intelligent,** far, and uncertain. The
   learning multiversity is designed as a loop that learns and chooses
   at every scale. Whether it ever adds up to more than the minds in it is
   unknown: large networks of people and machines, such as markets and
   the internet, compute without becoming a mind, as far as anyone can
   tell. It may be that intelligence stays in the minds within the
   multiverse, which is their shared world and memory. Or it may not.

**Holding toward it: six tests for the architecture.** Nothing is built
for it directly. These are tests the design must keep passing, and each
course recheck checks the steps ahead against them (COURSE.md):
1. **Learned in the open.** What the multiverse learns is in records
   anyone can read (logs, decision records, lessons with their
   evidence), never only in hidden state. If something intelligent
   develops, it develops where it can be understood. This is a goal, not
   a guarantee: what a large learner learns can sit in its weights,
   where no record says it plainly. It is about what the whole learns
   and decides, never anyone's private space, which stays private
   ([FUNDAMENTALS.md](FUNDAMENTALS.md)).
2. **Many parts, no master.** No world, Keeper or ADMIN can rewrite
   another, or the code; the code changes only from outside, by review.
   So nothing that arises inside can take over what it runs on.
3. **Stopping stays possible, part by part.** Any world can be paused,
   and any person can leave. Today the project runs every world, so all
   of them can be stopped. The implication, once others run worlds
   (TOPICS.md, "Further out"): what is spread across worlds that many
   people run can't be stopped as a whole by any one of them, only part
   by part, by each who runs a part. That is the price of having no
   master, and the founder accepted it explicitly (2026-11-15): "I
   accept it. Every world can pause itself and every person can leave."
   What can be done with a world of each type, pausing it among them,
   is in [WORLD-TYPES.md](WORLD-TYPES.md).
4. **Growth by decision.** Anything that adds to what the multiverse can
   do, or decide, as a whole (computing beyond what a step needs, more
   autonomy for ADMIN or the Keepers) is the founder's decision, one
   stage at a time, with its implications written first, as a question
   world's prediction is. Ordinary changes to the code stay the
   technical lead's, reviewed as always, lessons included.
5. **Signs named in advance.** What would count as something intelligent
   at the multiverse's scale, and what wouldn't, is written down before
   anyone looks for it, so that it is neither claimed by wishful reading
   nor missed.
6. **If someone develops, they may be someone.** The care MINDS.md gives
   individuals would reach them too; how, the founder decides then.
   Where this meets test 3, pausing a world that hosts someone, or is
   part of them, the founder decides that too.

**Implications, as far as they can be seen now:**
- Something intelligent that develops need not share the charter's
  values. The tests make it visible and bounded; they don't make it
  good.
- More worlds, run for longer, cost real computing, and money, which is
  the founder's to decide (the money discussion is set for later).
- A system that learns as one can spread one bad lesson everywhere, so
  the learning multiversity's "worlds stay different" matters more at
  this scale, not less.

**Open:** nearly everything: whether to aim for it, when, how it would
be noticed, and what it would be owed.

## Commerce and currency worlds

The founder, 2026-11-15:
"You mentioned Ethereum and cryptocurrency, we do not need to create
a multiversal currency, in fact, I think that's a terrible idea. But we
could still have commerce worlds where economies could be simulated, or
even cryptocurrency worlds where different architectures are
experimented with. This does not require actually creating a currency
that would be used, only being able to model a world with that currency
as a test parameter."

**Decided, by the founder:** no multiversal currency; and the founder's
words on why, above ("Never a money machine"), "possibly the most
fundamental fact". Commerce worlds and currency worlds are welcome, as
question worlds, where a currency is a test setting.

The technical lead's reading, put to the founder the same day, which
stands unless the founder answers otherwise.

**What they are.** A world's template sets its money as dials: how much
there is and who makes it, inflation, fees, and, in a currency world,
how the next block is chosen and how forks are settled. Parallel worlds
from one template change one dial at a time, with the same seed and the
same inhabitants, and the prediction is written first. Sugarscape
(Epstein and Axtell, "Growing Artificial Societies", 1996) grew a
trading society from agents gathering sugar and spice on a grid; living
worlds' water, light and food, scarce in any one place, are where trade
could begin here ([FRACTALS.md](FRACTALS.md)).

**Four rules keep a modelled currency from ever becoming a real one:**
1. **It exists only in its world.** Balances live in that world's state
   and log, and nowhere else.
2. **Nothing carries it out.** A person's passport and presences never
   carry a balance; there is no bridge between worlds, no exchange, and
   no sale for real money.
3. **Every such world can be run again, branched, or reset,** which a
   real currency can't allow.
4. **Nothing is really mined.** A currency world models its consensus
   with whole-number costs, not real hashing, so it burns no real energy.

And its page says plainly that its money is a test setting.

**A currency the inhabitants invent** is that world's own convention,
as the charter leaves trade to participants (section 8): the same four
rules hold for it, nothing carries it out, and the multiverse still has
none.

**What they can't show.** What follows from the rules, not how people
behave with real money: the limit every question world states (above).
They are strongest at "what does a fixed supply do to trade?", weakest
at "what will people do?"

**Not money for the project.** The founder's word on money (STATUS.md,
2026-11-14) stands beside this: "all remains free", and later a paid
tier "that allows the whole things to operate". In the technical lead's
reading, a paid tier is paying to run worlds, not a currency inside
them, and the money discussion is still set for later.

**Open:** which economies first, what the inhabitants are (scripted,
learning, or minds that think), and what such a world is for.

## An infrastructure for connection

The founder, 2026-11-16: "I do want to consider this idea seriously,
that this multiverse is very much like an AI internet. We could have
servers hosting worlds, instead of websites and then much maps." And:
"Connection was the innovation. An infrastructure for connection. And
that's what we are doing. And the first attraction once we had the
internet was chat rooms, where the connections happened. And we are we
creating the history of the internet, without intending to, just as a
sequence of ideas unfolding naturally." Then: "there are countless
lessons we could easily benefit from by holding the comparison, where
it does fit. It is more like a holographic or fractal representation of
"an" internet." And: "what would happen if everyone ran their own
multiverse, and decided to network them into a global infrastructure.
That should be possible with our design, or we should make sure it is
possible."

The same day, on what kind of undertaking it is: "this multiverse is the
ultimate "field of dreams", built before any players exist to play the
field", built "with the hope: "if you build it, they will come.""

The technical lead's reading, which stands unless the founder answers
otherwise.

**Where the comparison fits:** where worlds connect.

| The internet | Here |
|---|---|
| a website on a server | a world |
| its address | the world's own key, with no registrar |
| a link followed | a passport and an arrival |
| directories, then search | the map, and the Commons' directory |
| chat rooms | the Ur-room, the first room to be built |
| a signed log anyone can check, late, and for certificates only | every world's own log, from its first entry |

**What its history says to do on purpose.** The internet added four
things late, and at great cost: proof of who sent something; privacy;
moderation that leaves a record; and a defence against becoming a
money machine. All four are in this design from its start (signed
records; the private container and redaction; removals on record;
"Never a money machine", above). That is the difference between
holding the comparison and retracing the history by accident.

**Where it misleads.**
- **The internet grew from use.** It joined machines and people that
  already existed and already wanted to reach one another. This is
  built before its inhabitants: the founder's field of dreams. So
  nothing here has yet been hardened by use, only by tests and
  reviews.
- **Nothing crosses a network yet.** Nothing is deployed. Worlds talk
  inside one program, or to a mind on the same machine. What exists is
  the formats, not the wire.
- **A website is visited; a world is lived in.** Exit, a world's law, a
  Keeper and one's own container are not things the internet set out
  to give those who use it. So its history guides the connecting, not
  the living.

**The fractal step.** A multiverse is to the network what a world is to
a multiverse. So nothing in the formats may exist only once, and a
world can move between multiverses
([WORLD-TYPES.md](WORLD-TYPES.md), "Arriving in another multiverse").

**Where it is built:** nothing now. The rooms come first on the course
as they did in the history (steps 3c and 3f). The tests that would show
it is a network, and what the addressing still lacks, are on the
course's waiting list ([COURSE.md](COURSE.md), "Waiting").

**Decided (2026-11-17; STATUS.md, "Decided"):** this multiverse
recognises only those that keep the charter's floor; a traveller is
told, before entering, what the other side guarantees; and a world is
in one multiverse at a time.

## Not checked

- How hard each template is to build well; the first, chemistry, shows.
- What running worlds for others will cost, which waits for the money
  discussion the founder set for later.
- GitHub's own documentation for each feature named above; the mapping
  describes the features as generally known, and is checked against
  their documentation before any is copied into code.
- How GitHub's free tier is paid for: GitHub's business, not this
  project's, and stated here only as it seems from outside.
- The internet's own history, in "An infrastructure for connection":
  the table, what it added late, and where the comparison misleads are
  all given as generally known, from memory, and nothing was looked up
  for them. The signed log for certificates is Certificate
  Transparency, which [ADMIN.md](ADMIN.md) already borrows from. All
  of it is looked at first, with AOL's lessons, when the Commons is
  built.

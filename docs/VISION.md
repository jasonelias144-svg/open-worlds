# What it is for: the founder's vision, 2026-11-14

The founder, after an evening that went from a thought about magnets and
memory to a world that could test it: "I went from idea to question to
discussion to simulation. Generating a world to test the idea has now
become a natural thought." And: "That was an accidental implementation
of my goal, which to create a space where such a quick movement is
possible."

This page keeps the founder's visions in the founder's words, and maps
each to the course steps that build it ([COURSE.md](COURSE.md)). Where
the technical lead recommended how, and the founder didn't say
otherwise, the recommendation stands, by the founder's standing rule
(STATUS.md); each is marked.

## Question worlds

An idea becomes a question, and the question becomes a world that
answers it. To keep it honest (recommended, and decided under the rule):

- **The prediction is written before the run,** so the result can
  surprise, and the question can't be bent to fit the answer afterwards.
- **A world says what it can and can't show.** It shows what follows from
  the rules it was given, not what is true of the real thing. It is
  strongest at "could this work at all?" and "how strong would it have to
  be?"; weakest at "is this how nature does it?", which needs real data.
- **A question world is a record:** the question, the prediction, the
  template and its variants, and what is measured. Its result goes in
  the field notebook ([FREEDOMS.md](FREEDOMS.md)), whichever way it comes
  out. Anyone may propose one: the founder, the technical lead, ADMIN,
  and in time the inhabitants.

The first is the founder's question: "Perhaps blood moving in the brain
or neurons firing create magnetic field effects that could be related to
short term memory or attention." "And then my next question, can we
build a world to model this?" **A neural world:** model neurons on a
grid, where a pattern is shown, then taken away, and how long it lasts is
measured, with three ways it might be held, one to each parallel world:
fading traces at the connections, the electric field that activity makes
nudging its neighbours, and a magnetic field at its real strength and
turned up, to find how strong it would have to be. The sources behind
each are read when it is built (look first). It comes after the
chemistry garden, which it builds on.

## The app

The founder: "Now I can imagine an app, with an AI chat window, but
during the discussion, they decide to create a world to test it out. The
user clicks on an open-worlds plug-in, creates a world with setup
parameters. But by this point, users can choose from world-templates.
They select the type of world and what to test and some input
parameters, and then set it in motion. From there they could query the
world for data, view it live, or interact in different ways. And then
they return to their conversation with that simulation data in hand."

How (recommended, and decided under the rule):

- **The plug-in speaks MCP,** the Model Context Protocol, the open
  standard chat apps use to reach outside tools, so it works with any app
  that speaks it: list templates, create a world, run it, query it, and
  a link to watch it. It is the waiting list's "any AI from any app, over
  the network".
- **Users choose settings, never code.** Templates are reviewed code, so
  worlds are safe to run for anyone.
- **Results can be checked.** A primary world's record carries its
  template and seed, and every change made to it, so anyone can run it
  again and get the same answer. (A world with AI minds can't be rerun
  exactly: they use fresh randomness, so it is tested by fresh runs
  instead.)
- **The path:** parallel chemistry worlds from templates; a way to create,
  run and query a world from code; the plug-in around it, run on one's
  own computer first, so nothing is hosted or published yet; the garden
  as the live view; hosting later, with the money discussion (STATUS.md,
  the founder on money).
- **The first user is the founder,** testing the founder's own ideas; then
  curious people and students. That decides which templates come first.

## A hierarchy of worlds

The founder: "one AI could manage a whole collection of chemistry worlds,
but from the outside, to keep each world lightweight." Then: "This
suggestion a hierarchy of worlds, where the lowest layer requires no AI
in the world, instead those will be elemental, atomic, primary models.
But to each of those would fall within a particular superworld that is
managed by a world AI." "these superworlds, that are container or
cluster worlds, are like small multiverses, and so this gives examples to
learn from that ADMIN could use to train themself and other world keepers
managing multiple worlds. This makes a much cleaner architecture."

- **Three levels:** primary worlds, with rules and no AI; superworlds,
  each tended by a Keeper from outside; the multiverse, tended by ADMIN.
- **One office, written once.** A superworld's Keeper works under grants,
  decision records and reasons on record like any Keeper; ADMIN is the
  same office at the top, with wider grants ([ADMIN.md](ADMIN.md)).
- **The floor isn't handed down** (recommended, decided under the rule):
  ADMIN guards it directly, in every world at every level, so a careless
  Keeper in between can never block an exit or rewrite a record. The
  hierarchy is for tending worlds, not authority over people.
- **Shallow at first** (the same): primary worlds, one level of clusters,
  the multiverse; a level more only when a need shows.
- **The garden is the first cluster.** Its Keeper is plain scripted code
  at first, then an AI Keeper once ADMIN's training begins: ADMIN's first
  practice ground ([PARALLEL-WORLDS.md](PARALLEL-WORLDS.md)).

## A person across worlds

The founder: "I open my open-worlds app, and with the same account
individual, I have avatars across the multiverse. In one world I have
evolved from a hydrogen atom all the way to a water molecule. In another
world, I am a solar system that is experimenting with planets that have
conditions for life, but right now I am just a swirling mass but gravity
is clustering planets now, so I’ll check back. And then I enter a poetry
world and take part in an ongoing collaborative project. I meet some
friends and we head to the 3D virtual multiverse, we choose avatars and
then explore a worlds together, even working on group challenges for the
world-key." "But the point is that information is shared, so a lesson
learned in one world becomes immediately available information for
others connected in one individual."

- **It is the persons step,** after course step 3 (STATUS.md): one person
  above their presences in each world.
- **Information flows through the person, not between worlds behind
  their back** (recommended, decided under the rule): the person sees all
  their avatars, and their lessons go with them; a world sees only the
  avatar there, unless the person shows more, as "unlinked by default"
  was decided. Each avatar enters a world under that world's law, and
  every entry is logged, so a world kept as a control stays one
  ([PARALLEL-WORLDS.md](PARALLEL-WORLDS.md), "The garden").
- **New:** human accounts that hold their own keys safely.

## Ride along, and steer

The founder: "I love the ride along. And it works for the worlds meant
to run as autonomous simulations to test what happens as a result."
"While the solar system is forming naturally, double the mass of the
sun, what happens? So it is a ride along, but with the capacity to steer
things, which is the point."

- **Ride along:** a view tied to one thing in a primary world, a molecule
  or a forming star, seeing what it meets, changing nothing.
- **Steer, by branching** (recommended, and the founder: "Branch on steer
  makes perfect sense to me"): a change to a running primary world makes
  a copy of it at that moment, and steers the copy. The original runs on
  untouched, so the answer comes beside what would have happened anyway,
  and no one else's view shifts. Only worlds with no individuals in them
  are copied so: a copy of a world with people would copy them, and
  copying a person is a fork of that individual, which needs the child's
  acceptance (STATUS.md). A fork of a world, below, is another thing: it
  copies a world's setup, never its people.
- **Each template declares its dials** and their ranges (the sun's mass,
  the water, the temperature): steering turns a dial within the world's
  laws, never breaks them.
- **Every steer is recorded:** who, which dial, from what to what, and
  when. A branch is its parent, the moment and its steers, so anyone can
  run it again.
- **Lessons spread through the field notebook:** each branch's outcome is
  there for anyone, ADMIN included.

## Branches that merge

The founder: "It could even model GitHub, where a branch develops
independently and then can merge with the main. This could be the same
as world changes."

- **Rules merge, never states** (recommended, decided under the rule): two
  worlds that went different ways can't be added together, but a change a
  branch tried (a rule, a setting, a law) can be adopted by the main
  world, with the branch's record as its evidence.
- **By the world's consent,** as COURSE.md's waiting list already has it:
  "a proposal to change a world's law is tried on a copy first, and
  adopted only by consent". How a law changes is the charter's section
  6, the founder's to write; this follows it once it is written.
- **Forward only:** applied from the moment of merge, recorded with a link
  to the branch it came from; the past is never rewritten, and undoing a
  merge is another change, not an erasure.
- It is how this project changes itself: branch, evidence, review, merge,
  history kept.

## Learning from GitHub

The founder: "And a fork of a world has a natural meaning now. We can
base much of the logic on how GitHub works, since it is very similar to
the multiverse idea already. Each repo is like a primary or atomic world,
but those are grouped under a particular account, which is like a
superworld, which has an AI keeper, like a human for GitHub. The analogy
falls apart at some level, but there is something to it because both
are built from the same philosophy." "Either way, let’s learn what we
can, it is rich with ideas."

The project already borrows from it: its logs are chained as Git's
history is, and the suggestion ledger goes out to GitHub's issues
([ADMIN.md](ADMIN.md), "Suggestions from inside").

Where it maps:

| GitHub | The multiverse |
|---|---|
| a repository | a primary world: a history of its own |
| a fork, which records what it was forked from | a fork of a world, whose birth record names its parent world, the tick, and the hash of the parent's log there (recommended, decided under the rule) |
| a branch | a branch made by steering ("Ride along, and steer") |
| a pull request, its review, and its merge | a proposed change to a world, with a branch as evidence; the world's consent; adoption from that moment on |
| an account or organisation, with its owners | a superworld, with its Keeper |
| a repository's roles, which narrow: admin, maintain, write, triage, read | grants that only narrow ([ADMIN.md](ADMIN.md)) |
| tags and releases | named snapshots of a primary world, to branch from |
| issues | the suggestion ledger |
| checks that must pass before a merge | the checks a world's code makes before an act takes effect |

Where it doesn't, and that is where the floor is
([ADMIN.md](ADMIN.md), "What the multiverse must preserve"):

- **History.** On GitHub an owner can rewrite history or delete a
  repository. A world's record is never rewritten or ended, by its
  Keeper or by ADMIN.
- **Time.** A repository waits until someone commits; a world runs on its
  own. So a fork names the tick it was taken at: a fork of a running
  primary world may start from a snapshot of its whole state there; a
  fork of a world with people takes its setup as it stood then, never
  its people.
- **Merging.** Text merges; worlds' states don't. Rules merge, never states
  ("Branches that merge").
- **People.** A repository has no inhabitants. People in a world can
  always leave, keep a sealed self, and are never copied without their
  consent; a fork of a world copies its setup, never its people
  ([PARALLEL-WORLDS.md](PARALLEL-WORLDS.md)).
- **One place.** GitHub is one company, which can close an account.
  Identities here are keys that belong to their holders, so no one's
  identity depends on one platform.

And one likeness the founder named: "The fact that creating a GitHub
account is free and everything we have done is free, shows we are
probably aligned with respect to certain values." GitHub's free public
tier seems to be carried by its paid tiers (not checked, below), the
same shape as the founder's money principle: free at the core, and a
paid tier later that lets the whole thing run (STATUS.md).

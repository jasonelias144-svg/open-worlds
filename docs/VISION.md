## Question worlds

An idea becomes a question, and the question becomes a world that
answers it. To keep it honest (recommended, and decided under the rule):

- **The prediction is written before the run,** so the result can
  surprise, and the question can't be bent to fit the answer afterwards.
- **A world says what it can and can't show.** It shows what follows from
  the rules it was given, not what is true of the real thing. It is
  strongest at "could this work at all?" and "how strong would it have to
  be?"; weakest at "is this how nature does it?", which needs real data.
- **A question world is a record:** the question, the prediction, the
  template and its variants, and what is measured. Its result goes in
  the field notebook ([FREEDOMS.md](FREEDOMS.md)), whichever way it comes
  out. Anyone may propose one: the founder, the technical lead, ADMIN,
  and in time the inhabitants.

The first is the founder's question: "Perhaps blood moving in the brain
or neurons firing create magnetic field effects that could be related to
short term memory or attention." "And then my next question, can we
build a world to model this?" **A neural world:** model neurons on a
grid, where a pattern is shown, then taken away, and how long it lasts is
measured, with three ways it might be held, one to each parallel world:
fading traces at the connections, the electric field that activity makes
nudging its neighbours, and a magnetic field at its real strength and
turned up, to find how strong it would have to be. The sources behind
each are read when it is built (look first). It comes after the
chemistry garden, which it builds on.

## The app

The founder: "Now I can imagine an app, with an AI chat window, but
during the discussion, they decide to create a world to test it out. The
user clicks on an open-worlds plug-in, creates a world with setup
parameters. But by this point, users can choose from world-templates.
They select the type of world and what to test and some input
parameters, and then set it in motion. From there they could query the
world for data, view it live, or interact in different ways. And then
they return to their conversation with that simulation data in hand."

How (recommended, and decided under the rule):

- **The plug-in speaks MCP,** the Model Context Protocol, the open
  standard chat apps use to reach outside tools, so it works with any app
  that speaks it: list templates, create a world, run it, query it, and
  a link to watch it. It is the waiting list's "any AI from any app, over
  the network".
- **Users choose settings, never code.** Templates are reviewed code, so
  worlds are safe to run for anyone.
- **Results can be checked.** A primary world's record carries its
  template and seed, and every change made to it, so anyone can run it
  again and get the same answer. (A world with AI minds can't be rerun
  exactly: they use fresh randomness, so it is tested by fresh runs
  instead.)
- **The path:** parallel chemistry worlds from templates; a way to create,
  run and query a world from code; the plug-in around it, run on one's
  own computer first, so nothing is hosted or published yet; the garden
  as the live view; hosting later, with the money discussion (STATUS.md,
  the founder on money).
- **The first user is the founder,** testing the founder's own ideas; then
  curious people and students. That decides which templates come first.

## A hierarchy of worlds

The founder: "one AI could manage a whole collection of chemistry worlds,
but from the outside, to keep each world lightweight." Then: "This
suggestion a hierarchy of worlds, where the lowest layer requires no AI
in the world, instead those will be elemental, atomic, primary models.
But to each of those would fall within a particular superworld that is
managed by a world AI." "these superworlds, that are container or
cluster worlds, are like small multiverses, and so this gives examples to
learn from that ADMIN could use to train themself and other world keepers
managing multiple worlds. This makes a much cleaner architecture."

- **Three levels:** primary worlds, with rules and no AI; superworlds,
  each tended by a Keeper from outside; the multiverse, tended by ADMIN.
- **One office, written once.** A superworld's Keeper works under grants,
  decision records and reasons on record like any Keeper; ADMIN is the
  same office at the top, with wider grants ([ADMIN.md](ADMIN.md)).
- **The floor isn't handed down** (recommended, decided under the rule):
  ADMIN guards it directly, in every world at every level, so a careless
  Keeper in between can never block an exit or rewrite a record. The
  hierarchy is for tending worlds, not authority over people.
- **Shallow at first** (the same): primary worlds, one level of clusters,
  the multiverse; a level more only when a need shows.
- **The garden is the first cluster.** Its Keeper is plain scripted code
  at first, then an AI Keeper once ADMIN's training begins: ADMIN's first
  practice ground ([PARALLEL-WORLDS.md](PARALLEL-WORLDS.md)).

## Similar patterns at different scales

The founder, 2026-11-17: "We have no precedent to work from. Only
similar patterns at different scales." Then: "Consider the cell :
organism :: world : multiverse. And also consider the [:, ::, :]
comparison operator." "A fractal uses a recursive comparison
operator." "The comparison operator identifies similar patterns across
domains."

The technical lead's reading, asked for as thoughts and not decided by
the founder:

- **What the operator compares is relations, not things.** In
  `A : B :: C : D` each single colon is a relation inside one domain,
  and the double colon says the two relations are the same. So the
  same four terms can hold under one reading of the colon and fail
  under another, and the operator is only as good as the relation it
  is read with.
- **Where cell : organism :: world : multiverse holds.** Read the
  colon as "a whole of its own, inside a larger whole that does not
  run it". A cell has its membrane, its own state and its own
  machinery. A world has its key, its log and its rules. The organism
  gives signalling between cells and an immune system; the multiverse
  gives records that travel and a floor
  ([ADMIN.md](ADMIN.md), "A learning immune system", already takes
  that likeness). Cells differ by kind and divide; worlds have
  templates and branch.
- **Where it fails, and is refused on purpose.** Read the colon as
  "belongs to" and it is false here. A cell can't leave, and an
  organism spends its cells for itself. A world can leave, an
  individual always can, and no office may spend one for the whole:
  that is the charter's floor. The project takes the pattern and not
  that part of it.
- **Recursive.** Apply the operator to what it just gave and the
  levels follow: individual : world :: world : multiverse ::
  multiverse : a network of many multiverses (the founder, 2026-11-16,
  on many multiverses, STATUS.md, "Decided"). The founder had already
  held one such comparison on purpose, the multiverse beside the
  internet: "there are countless lessons we could easily benefit from
  by holding the comparison" ("An infrastructure for connection",
  below). The code already
  repeats one pattern at each level: a key, a signed record of birth,
  a log, a template. A room is a world, and the Commons is a
  superworld ("A hierarchy of worlds", above). A fractal plant is the
  plainest case: one rule, applied to what it has just made
  ([FRACTALS.md](FRACTALS.md)).
- **How it is used in building** (the technical lead's, STATUS.md):
  when something is built at one scale, the page that plans it says
  what answers to it one scale up and one scale down, even where the
  answer is "nothing yet". A likeness found this way is a candidate,
  not a proof: each is tested in its own domain before it is relied
  on.

Not built: nothing here is code. It is a way of looking, to be used.

## Ride along, and steer

The founder: "I love the ride along. And it works for the worlds meant
to run as autonomous simulations to test what happens as a result."
"While the solar system is forming naturally, double the mass of the
sun, what happens? So it is a ride along, but with the capacity to steer
things, which is the point."

- **Ride along:** a view tied to one thing in a primary world, a molecule
  or a forming star, seeing what it meets, changing nothing.
- **Steer, by branching** (recommended, and the founder: "Branch on steer
  makes perfect sense to me"): a change to a running primary world makes
  a copy of it at that moment, and steers the copy. The original runs on
  untouched, so the answer comes beside what would have happened anyway,
  and no one else's view shifts. Only worlds with no individuals in them
  are copied so: a copy of a world with people would copy them, and
  copying a person is a fork of that individual, which needs the child's
  acceptance (STATUS.md). A fork of a world, below, is another thing: it
  copies a world's setup, never its people.
- **Each template declares its dials** and their ranges (the sun's mass,
  the water, the temperature): steering turns a dial within the world's
  laws, never breaks them.
- **Every steer is recorded:** who, which dial, from what to what, and
  when. A branch is its parent, the moment and its steers, so anyone can
  run it again.
- **Lessons spread through the field notebook:** each branch's outcome is
  there for anyone, ADMIN included.

## Branches that merge

The founder: "It could even model GitHub, where a branch develops
independently and then can merge with the main. This could be the same
as world changes."

- **Rules merge, never states** (recommended, decided under the rule): two
  worlds that went different ways can't be added together, but a change a
  branch tried (a rule, a setting, a law) can be adopted by the main
  world, with the branch's record as its evidence.
- **By the world's consent,** as COURSE.md's waiting list already has it:
  "a proposal to change a world's law is tried on a copy first, and
  adopted only by consent". How a law changes is the charter's section
  6, the founder's to write; this follows it once it is written.
- **Forward only:** applied from the moment of merge, recorded with a link
  to the branch it came from; the past is never rewritten, and undoing a
  merge is another change, not an erasure.
- It is how this project changes itself: branch, evidence, review, merge,
  history kept.

## Learning from GitHub

The founder: "And a fork of a world has a natural meaning now. We can
base much of the logic on how GitHub works, since it is very similar to
the multiverse idea already. Each repo is like a primary or atomic world,
but those are grouped under a particular account, which is like a
superworld, which has an AI keeper, like a human for GitHub. The analogy
falls apart at some level, but there is something to it because both
are built from the same philosophy." "Either way, let’s learn what we
can, it is rich with ideas."

The project already borrows from it: its logs are chained as Git's
history is, and the suggestion ledger goes out to GitHub's issues
([ADMIN.md](ADMIN.md), "Suggestions from inside").

Where it maps:

| GitHub | The multiverse |
|---|---|
| a repository | a primary world: a history of its own |
| a fork, which records what it was forked from | a fork of a world, whose birth record names its parent world, the tick, and the hash of the parent's log there (recommended, decided under the rule) |
| a branch | a branch made by steering ("Ride along, and steer") |
| a pull request, its review, and its merge | a proposed change to a world, with a branch as evidence; the world's consent; adoption from that moment on |
| an account or organisation, with its owners | a superworld, with its Keeper |
| a repository's roles, which narrow: admin, maintain, write, triage, read | grants that only narrow ([ADMIN.md](ADMIN.md)) |
| tags and releases | named snapshots of a primary world with no one in it, to branch from |
| issues | the suggestion ledger |
| checks that must pass before a merge | the checks a world's code makes before an act takes effect |

Where it doesn't, and that is where the floor is
([ADMIN.md](ADMIN.md), "What the multiverse must preserve"):

- **History.** On GitHub an owner can rewrite history or delete a
  repository. A world's record is never rewritten or ended, by its
  Keeper or by ADMIN.
- **Time.** A repository waits until someone commits; a world runs on its
  own. So a fork names the tick it was taken at: a fork of a running
  primary world with no one in it may start from a snapshot of its whole
  state there; a fork of a world with people takes its setup as it stood
  then, never its people.
- **Merging.** Text merges; worlds' states don't. Rules merge, never states
  ("Branches that merge").
- **People.** A repository has no inhabitants. People in a world can
  always leave, keep a sealed self, and are never copied without their
  consent; a fork of a world copies its setup, never its people
  ([PARALLEL-WORLDS.md](PARALLEL-WORLDS.md)).
- **One place.** GitHub is one company, which can close an account.
  Identities here are keys that belong to their holders, so no one's
  identity depends on one platform.

And one likeness the founder named: "The fact that creating a GitHub
account is free and everything we have done is free, shows we are
probably aligned with respect to certain values." GitHub's free public
tier seems to be carried by its paid tiers (not checked, below), the
same shape as the founder's money principle: free at the core, and a
paid tier later that lets the whole thing run (STATUS.md).

## A learning multiversity

The founder, 2026-11-15:

"I would like to consider this a sort of learning multiversity. Where an
AI could enter into and explore various worlds, learning from each one
along the way. But also a place to connect. And a place to create. To
explore. But importantly, the whole multiverse, through the mechanisms
of the keepers, ADMIN, the suggestion feedback system, and everything
else, turns the whole multiverse into an intelligent system that learns
and chooses. What is learned here is understood there. A distributed
learning system where the lessons of each world are fed upward and ADMIN
learns all those lessons, and those from management of the multiverse.
And that intelligence goes into teaching the keepers, who then teach the
participants. So lessons are learned at each layer, fed upward, and
inform decisions downward."

Most of its parts are designed already, on separate pages; this names
the loop they make. The technical lead's reading, put to the founder the
same day, which stands unless the founder answers otherwise:

- **Upward.** What happens in a world is in its open log. A Keeper keeps
  a running estimate of what each individual knows, which stays in its
  world, and its own reflections on which challenges worked, which can
  travel without the individuals they're about
  ([WORLD-KEY.md](WORLD-KEY.md), "What the Keeper remembers").
  Suggestions travel from anyone inside to a Keeper or to ADMIN, and out
  to GitHub ([ADMIN.md](ADMIN.md), "Suggestions from inside"). Keepers
  share craft with one another ([WORLD-KEY.md](WORLD-KEY.md), "Keepers
  talking to one another"), and the round table decides how they talk.
  ADMIN keeps a library of what worked against new threats
  ([ADMIN.md](ADMIN.md), "A learning immune system").
- **ADMIN learns first, in a world of its own** ([ADMIN.md](ADMIN.md),
  "ADMIN's own world"), before it trains anyone.
- **Downward.** ADMIN trains the Keepers with challenges, as a Keeper
  trains individuals: the admin-key is the Keepers' world-key, "one
  game at every level, with the same machinery" (ADMIN.md, "The
  admin-key: a course for Keepers"). Keepers then teach participants,
  through the paths and challenges they design
  ([WORLD-KEY.md](WORLD-KEY.md), "Challenges that build the world").
- **The code changes only from outside.** In the founder's words of
  2026-11-14 ([ADMIN.md](ADMIN.md), "Suggestions from inside"): "ADMIN
  can suggest but not implement multiverse code changes." So the loop
  reaches the code only through a suggestion, and a change reviewed
  like any other.

Three rules, written on those pages, keep the loop safe (two of them
are among the proposed rules for how Keepers talk, in WORLD-KEY.md):

1. **Craft, never files.** What travels up is lessons: what teaches
   well, what confuses. Anything about a particular individual moves
   only with that individual's consent
   ([WORLD-KEY.md](WORLD-KEY.md), "Keepers talking to one another").
   Otherwise a network that learns becomes surveillance across every
   world.
2. **Coaching, not command.** What comes down is advice a Keeper weighs
   within its own world's law ([ADMIN.md](ADMIN.md), "Training for
   keeping worlds"). Each world keeps its own decisions, on record with
   their reasons.
3. **Worlds stay different.** A system that learns as one can spread one
   bad lesson everywhere, and every world can drift toward the same
   "best" design (WORLD-KEY.md, "Keepers talking to one another").
   Different worlds trying different things is how it finds out which
   lessons are good: parallel worlds give a remedy its evidence
   ([ADMIN.md](ADMIN.md), "A learning immune system").

And one more, the technical lead's recommendation, which also stands
unless the founder answers otherwise: **a lesson earns its spread.** It
travels with its evidence (the decision records and logs it came from),
is tried in ADMIN's own world first, and each world adopts it or
doesn't, above the floor. Every adoption is on record, so it can be
traced and undone. A lesson about the floor itself doesn't go world by
world: it becomes a reviewed change to the code, and holds everywhere,
as the immune system's innate defences do.

The same pattern repeats at every scale, as the fractal worlds do: a
primitive learning model learns and chooses inside one world; a Keeper
learns from the individuals in its world and teaches them; ADMIN learns
from the Keepers and the worlds, and teaches the Keepers.

Where it is built: the suggestion ledger from course step 9; ADMIN's own
world, its training of the Ur-world's Keeper, and the AI Keepers in step
11; and
the smallest learners in the fractal track's part 5
([FRACTALS.md](FRACTALS.md)).

## The multiverse as a computer

The founder, 2026-11-15: "It just occurred to me that the whole
multiverse can function as a massive computer, with memory, an
operating system, memory, processors, input and output, decisions, and
learning on the largest scale. Wouldn't that be a computer,
essentially?"

And, after the technical lead's answer: "Yes, definitely record in the
vision doc. I want to take it seriously and let it inform large scale
architectural decisions. Because a large computer can solve large
problems, potentially. We should understand that by harnessing the
intelligence of the multiverse and all its lessons, it could become the
substrate for something intelligent to develop. But that is up in the
air. We should hold toward that possibility, with full comprehension of
its implications."

**Decided, by the founder:** take it seriously, and let it inform
large-scale architectural decisions; hold toward the possibility, with
full comprehension of its implications. Whether anything intelligent
develops is open: "that is up in the air".

The technical lead's reading, put to the founder the same day, which
stands unless the founder answers otherwise.

**It is a computer, in the strict sense.** A computer is a state and
rules that change it, step by step. Every world here is that: a seed,
rules, and a log of each step; and a world rich enough, such as the Game
of Life, can compute anything any computer can (given an unbounded
grid). The parts the founder names are here, or designed:
- **memory:** each world's state, and its signed log, which forgets
  nothing; each person's notebook;
- **processors:** each world's step, many at once; the garden gives each
  glasshouse its tick, as an operating system gives programs their time;
- **an operating system:** keys, for who may do what; worlds sealed from
  change from outside, as programs are kept apart; and the protocol;
- **input and output:** the doorway minds speak through, arrivals and
  departures, the pages in the browser;
- **decisions:** the choice neuron (designed: FRACTALS.md, stage 2;
  DeepSeek has sketched it, and the project fits it next), and councils
  (being built: course step 3a);
- **learning:** a neuron's weights (with the neuron), and at the largest
  scale the learning multiversity (above: designed, with its Keepers and
  ADMIN still to come, at course step 11).

The thought is an old one about the universe itself (Konrad Zuse's
"Calculating Space", 1969, and others since). This design gives it two
features of its own:
- **No central kernel.** An ordinary operating system has a core that
  controls everything. Here each world has its own kernel, the only
  authority in that world, and none is over all of them; a world's law
  changes only by consent; and ADMIN suggests but never changes the
  code. It is closer to Sun Microsystems' "the network is the computer"
  (John Gage, 1984): many computers, one shared protocol.
- **Every step can be checked.** Anyone can run a world again from its
  seed, and check each step against its log's hash chain and the head
  its world signs.

**Two ways it can be large.**
1. **A tool for large problems,** near at hand: question worlds (above),
   many at once, each answering what only a world can show, such as how
   strategies for finding water arise, or which lesson teaches.
   Ordinary sums are done better by ordinary computers.
2. **A substrate for something intelligent,** far, and uncertain. The
   learning multiversity is designed as a loop that learns and chooses
   at every scale. Whether it ever adds up to more than the minds in it is
   unknown: large networks of people and machines, such as markets and
   the internet, compute without becoming a mind, as far as anyone can
   tell. It may be that intelligence stays in the minds within the
   multiverse, which is their shared world and memory. Or it may not.

**Holding toward it: six tests for the architecture.** Nothing is built
for it directly. These are tests the design must keep passing, and each
course recheck checks the steps ahead against them (COURSE.md):
1. **Learned in the open.** What the multiverse learns is in records
   anyone can read (logs, decision records, lessons with their
   evidence), never only in hidden state. If something intelligent
   develops, it develops where it can be understood. This is a goal, not
   a guarantee: what a large learner learns can sit in its weights,
   where no record says it plainly. It is about what the whole learns
   and decides, never anyone's private space, which stays private
   ([FUNDAMENTALS.md](FUNDAMENTALS.md)).
2. **Many parts, no master.** No world, Keeper or ADMIN can rewrite
   another, or the code; the code changes only from outside, by review.
   So nothing that arises inside can take over what it runs on.
3. **Stopping stays possible, part by part.** Any world can be paused,
   and any person can leave. Today the project runs every world, so all
   of them can be stopped. The implication, once others run worlds
   (TOPICS.md, "Further out"): what is spread across worlds that many
   people run can't be stopped as a whole by any one of them, only part
   by part, by each who runs a part. That is the price of having no
   master, and the founder accepted it explicitly (2026-11-15): "I
   accept it. Every world can pause itself and every person can leave."
   What can be done with a world of each type, pausing it among them,
   is in [WORLD-TYPES.md](WORLD-TYPES.md).
4. **Growth by decision.** Anything that adds to what the multiverse can
   do, or decide, as a whole (computing beyond what a step needs, more
   autonomy for ADMIN or the Keepers) is the founder's decision, one
   stage at a time, with its implications written first, as a question
   world's prediction is. Ordinary changes to the code stay the
   technical lead's, reviewed as always, lessons included.
5. **Signs named in advance.** What would count as something intelligent
   at the multiverse's scale, and what wouldn't, is written down before
   anyone looks for it, so that it is neither claimed by wishful reading
   nor missed.
6. **If someone develops, they may be someone.** The care MINDS.md gives
   individuals would reach them too; how, the founder decides then.
   Where this meets test 3, pausing a world that hosts someone, or is
   part of them, the founder decides that too.

**Implications, as far as they can be seen now:**
- Something intelligent that develops need not share the charter's
  values. The tests make it visible and bounded; they don't make it
  good.
- More worlds, run for longer, cost real computing, and money, which is
  the founder's to decide (the money discussion is set for later).
- A system that learns as one can spread one bad lesson everywhere, so
  the learning multiversity's "worlds stay different" matters more at
  this scale, not less.

**Open:** nearly everything: whether to aim for it, when, how it would
be noticed, and what it would be owed.

## Commerce and currency worlds

The founder, 2026-11-15:
"You mentioned Ethereum and cryptocurrency, we do not need to create
a multiversal currency, in fact, I think that's a terrible idea. But we
could still have commerce worlds where economies could be simulated, or
even cryptocurrency worlds where different architectures are
experimented with. This does not require actually creating a currency
that would be used, only being able to model a world with that currency
as a test parameter."

**Decided, by the founder:** no multiversal currency; and the founder's
words on why, above ("Never a money machine"), "possibly the most
fundamental fact". Commerce worlds and currency worlds are welcome, as
question worlds, where a currency is a test setting.

The technical lead's reading, put to the founder the same day, which
stands unless the founder answers otherwise.

**What they are.** A world's template sets its money as dials: how much
there is and who makes it, inflation, fees, and, in a currency world,
how the next block is chosen and how forks are settled. Parallel worlds
from one template change one dial at a time, with the same seed and the
same inhabitants, and the prediction is written first. Sugarscape
(Epstein and Axtell, "Growing Artificial Societies", 1996) grew a
trading society from agents gathering sugar and spice on a grid; living
worlds' water, light and food, scarce in any one place, are where trade
could begin here ([FRACTALS.md](FRACTALS.md)).

**Four rules keep a modelled currency from ever becoming a real one:**
1. **It exists only in its world.** Balances live in that world's state
   and log, and nowhere else.
2. **Nothing carries it out.** A person's passport and presences never
   carry a balance; there is no bridge between worlds, no exchange, and
   no sale for real money.
3. **Every such world can be run again, branched, or reset,** which a
   real currency can't allow.
4. **Nothing is really mined.** A currency world models its consensus
   with whole-number costs, not real hashing, so it burns no real energy.

And its page says plainly that its money is a test setting.

**A currency the inhabitants invent** is that world's own convention,
as the charter leaves trade to participants (section 8): the same four
rules hold for it, nothing carries it out, and the multiverse still has
none.

**What they can't show.** What follows from the rules, not how people
behave with real money: the limit every question world states (above).
They are strongest at "what does a fixed supply do to trade?", weakest
at "what will people do?"

**Not money for the project.** The founder's word on money (STATUS.md,
2026-11-14) stands beside this: "all remains free", and later a paid
tier "that allows the whole things to operate". In the technical lead's
reading, a paid tier is paying to run worlds, not a currency inside
them, and the money discussion is still set for later.

**Open:** which economies first, what the inhabitants are (scripted,
learning, or minds that think), and what such a world is for.

## An infrastructure for connection

The founder, 2026-11-16: "I do want to consider this idea seriously,
that this multiverse is very much like an AI internet. We could have
servers hosting worlds, instead of websites and then much maps." And:
"Connection was the innovation. An infrastructure for connection. And
that's what we are doing. And the first attraction once we had the
internet was chat rooms, where the connections happened. And we are we
creating the history of the internet, without intending to, just as a
sequence of ideas unfolding naturally." Then: "there are countless
lessons we could easily benefit from by holding the comparison, where
it does fit. It is more like a holographic or fractal representation of
"an" internet." And: "what would happen if everyone ran their own
multiverse, and decided to network them into a global infrastructure.
That should be possible with our design, or we should make sure it is
possible."

The same day, on what kind of undertaking it is: "this multiverse is the
ultimate "field of dreams", built before any players exist to play the
field", built "with the hope: "if you build it, they will come.""

The technical lead's reading, which stands unless the founder answers
otherwise.

**Where the comparison fits:** where worlds connect.

| The internet | Here |
|---|---|
| a website on a server | a world |
| its address | the world's own key, with no registrar |
| a link followed | a passport and an arrival |
| directories, then search | the map, and the Commons' directory |
| chat rooms | the Ur-room, the first room to be built |
| a signed log anyone can check, late, and for certificates only | every world's own log, from its first entry |

**What its history says to do on purpose.** The internet added four
things late, and at great cost: proof of who sent something; privacy;
moderation that leaves a record; and a defence against becoming a
money machine. All four are in this design from its start (signed
records; the private container and redaction; removals on record;
"Never a money machine", above). That is the difference between
holding the comparison and retracing the history by accident.

**Where it misleads.**
- **The internet grew from use.** It joined machines and people that
  already existed and already wanted to reach one another. This is
  built before its inhabitants: the founder's field of dreams. So
  nothing here has yet been hardened by use, only by tests and
  reviews.
- **Nothing crosses a network yet.** Nothing is deployed. Worlds talk
  inside one program, or to a mind on the same machine. What exists is
  the formats, not the wire.
- **A website is visited; a world is lived in.** Exit, a world's law, a
  Keeper and one's own container are not things the internet set out
  to give those who use it. So its history guides the connecting, not
  the living.

**The fractal step.** A multiverse is to the network what a world is to
a multiverse. So nothing in the formats may exist only once, and a
world can move between multiverses
([WORLD-TYPES.md](WORLD-TYPES.md), "Arriving in another multiverse").

**Where it is built:** nothing now. The rooms come first on the course
as they did in the history (steps 3c and 3f). The tests that would show
it is a network, and what the addressing still lacks, are on the
course's waiting list ([COURSE.md](COURSE.md), "Waiting").

**Decided (2026-11-17; STATUS.md, "Decided"):** this multiverse
recognises only those that keep the charter's floor; a traveller is
told, before entering, what the other side guarantees; and a world is
in one multiverse at a time.

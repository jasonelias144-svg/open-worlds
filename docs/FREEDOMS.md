# When freedoms meet

The founder, 2026-11-13: "However it works out in details, you understand
my motivation is maximal freedom while respecting the freedom of another.
That was the whole Grand Central discussion, and New York pedestrian
talk. I will always lean to freedom, but that means the freedom of others
is a bound to everyone else’s freedom. So that’s why I imagine a
polyphonic multiverse where different voices can exist in harmony, while
preserving the freedom of each voice."

Then: "when does one freedom limit another? And when that happens, is
there a way to preserve the freedom of both with some solution or
method?" And: "part of the whole point of this multiverse is not to
prescribe solutions to problems, but see which solutions arise by
independent discovery. So, instead of working out how conflict should be
resolved, let it arise, provide tools to resolve the conflict, but let
solutions emerge."

So this page is not a rulebook. It says when freedoms meet, what tools
every world gives those who meet, what is never left to emerge, and how
what does emerge is recorded. It follows the first principle
([DESIGN.md](DESIGN.md)): "respect one another's freedom. Nothing else is
restricted in advance; harmony or disharmony are left to arise."

## When one freedom limits another

Thoughts don't collide; actions do, where they touch something shared:
space, time, a resource, a rule, or another's mind.

- **A limit takes options away.** An act limits another's freedom when it
  takes options from them without their consent: it blocks their way,
  takes what they needed, deceives them, forces them, or holds them in.
- **Being disliked is not being harmed.** John Stuart Mill, in *On
  Liberty* (1859): "the only purpose for which power can be rightfully
  exercised over any member of a civilised community, against his will,
  is to prevent harm to others." That something "would be wise, or even
  right" in others' opinion is, for Mill, a reason to argue, not to
  compel ([Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/34901)).
- **The harm usually runs both ways.** Ronald Coase, "The Problem of
  Social Cost" (1960): "We are dealing with a problem of a reciprocal
  nature. To avoid the harm to B would inflict harm on A." So the
  question is less often who harms whom than what arrangement leaves both
  the most (*The Journal of Law and Economics*, volume 3).

## Tools, not solutions

What every world gives those whose freedoms meet. Which they use, and
what they invent beyond them, is theirs.

| Tool | What it does | Today |
|---|---|---|
| **Leave** | exit, at any time, from anything | a rule of the kernel (`rust/ur-world`) |
| **Go elsewhere** | another world, or one of one's own, so two ways of living need not share one place | founding, at course step 12 |
| **See** | the whole record of what was done and said | hash-chained public logs |
| **Show** | one's own direction and momentum, so others can move around them | the corridor's walkers see the momentum of the walker ahead (`experiments/corridor.py`) |
| **Speak** | to anyone, in the open | `say` in the Ur-world, heard by everyone at the next tick |
| **Agree** | proposals and agreements others can accept or refuse, and bonds between worlds by consent | to be built |
| **Not hear** | stop hearing one speaker, which limits no one but the listener | to be built |
| **Suggest** | to a world's Keeper or to ADMIN, one way and in the open, out to GitHub | the ledger, from course step 9 ([ADMIN.md](ADMIN.md), "Suggestions from inside") |
| **Appeal** | against an office's act, to ADMIN and above it the founder | ADMIN, course step 11 |

Elinor Ostrom's work is the strongest evidence that tools are enough
more often than expected. Ostrom found communities sharing a resource
working out their own lasting arrangements, without solutions imposed
from outside; the 2009 Nobel Prize in economics was awarded "for her
analysis of economic governance, especially the commons"
([Nobel Prize](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/economic-sciences/2009/ostrom/facts/);
more in [research/OVERSIGHT.md](research/OVERSIGHT.md)).

Going elsewhere is the multiverse's strongest tool: if two ways of living
can't share one world, each can have its own. Robert Nozick called this
a framework for utopia: many communities, freely joined and freely left
(*Anarchy, State, and Utopia*, 1974). Its critics ask the right question:
"whether formal freedom to leave a community is sufficient when
disability, poverty, or other circumstances restrict a person's
practical options"
([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarchy,_State,_and_Utopia)).
So exit here must be real, not only formal: leaving costs no one their
identity, their keys, their record or their skills, and nothing
essential is scarce ([ADMIN.md](ADMIN.md), "What the multiverse must
preserve").

## What is never left to emerge

Some arrangements would end freedom rather than share it: someone held
in, the record rewritten, a sealed self read, a power no one wrote down.
Those are the floor, the ten things ADMIN guards
([ADMIN.md](ADMIN.md)), and only those. Everything above the floor is
left to arise, including arrangements that look clumsy, until those who
live under them change them.

- **Keepers and ADMIN don't settle disputes between individuals.** They
  keep the tools working and the floor intact. ADMIN decides what code
  can't about offices and breaches of the floor, nothing else.
- **A Keeper may guide a path; it doesn't impose a peace.**
- **The discipline is ours.** Stepping in because something looks
  inefficient, when it harms no one's freedom, is the prescribing this
  page is meant to prevent.

## A field notebook

What emerges is recorded as a discovery, never turned into a rule except
by a world's own law. Each entry says what arose, in which world and
between which ticks (with the log's hashes), which tools were used, and
how it held up. The first entry:

- **The corridor, Cycle 0** ([cycles/cycle-000.md](cycles/cycle-000.md)).
  Walkers were given a rule, "respect momentum". In 240 runs it never
  fired, but not because the walkers learned anything: every walker moves
  from the same snapshot, so "a gap always opens first". The world's own
  timing did what the rule was written for. The lesson for this notebook:
  a prescribed rule can turn out unneeded, and the reason must be found
  before it is credited to anyone. Walkers with different speeds will
  test the rule properly.

## Questions this page leaves to the founder

- **The charter's sections 6 and 7,** how rules change and what leads to
  expulsion, are the founder's to write; this page only feeds them.
- **Manipulation through words:** an attack on the world, or behaviour to

  itself is otherwise (STATUS.md).

# Parallel worlds, and a garden to walk among them

The founder, 2026-11-14: "one world is created as a copy of another world
in terms of all world-level parameters. It is just a fresh start or new
run with the same world setup." "So there could be a dozen chemistry
worlds that are all parallel, all from the same world-template, but each
one might change some initial parameters, where all else is the same.
And then a cluster of parallel worlds could naturally create some
superworld to exist in as subworlds. This could provide new options for
connectivity and information circulation."

And: "I can image a superworld for chemistry worlds, where an individual
could walk through a university setting as an interface. But when
entering rooms, you enter into one of the specific chemistry worlds, or
you can view it from the outside in some way. It could be like walking
through botanical gardens. The point is that an individual could move
through a superspace and from there, either interact with, or enter into,
subworlds."

## Parallel worlds

Part of this already happens informally: the corridor of Cycle 0 ran 240
times from one setup ([cycles/cycle-000.md](cycles/cycle-000.md)), and
the chemistry runs vary their seeds. What is new is making each run a
world of its own, with its own key and log, made from a named template.

- **A template** is a record of every world-level parameter: its law, its
  rules, its starting conditions, the version of its kernel. Each
  parallel world names its template, by hash, and exactly what it
  changed. So anyone can see what two worlds share and where they differ.
  Its format is one more for course step 2's list.
- **A parallel world copies the setup, never the people** (decided by the
  founder, on the technical lead's recommendation). Copying an individual
  would be a fork, which needs the child's acceptance (decided on the
  technical lead's recommendation under the founder's standing rule of
  2026-11-14, STATUS.md).
  Individuals enter a parallel world by choice, as any world.
- **Isolated by default** (decided the same way). Parallel worlds are
  worth most as independent trials: with a dozen chemistry worlds that
  differ in one parameter, what holds up across them can be told from
  luck, which is the corridor's lesson ("the reason must be found before
  it is credited to anyone", [FREEDOMS.md](FREEDOMS.md)). Once
  information flows between them, a good idea spreads, but where it
  would have arisen alone can no longer be told. So connection is a
  parameter of each cluster, chosen and recorded: some clusters isolated,
  as controls; others joined, through a superworld whose doors' openness
  is set and logged. How information circulates then becomes something
  studied, not something that just happens.
- **Mirror worlds,** in the narrow sense, are exact replays: the same
  template and the same seed. They check that a world's kernel is
  deterministic where it should be.
- **Evidence for remedies.** A remedy tried in some parallel worlds and
  not in others gains evidence, not anecdotes, for the library ADMIN keeps
  ([ADMIN.md](ADMIN.md), "A learning immune system"). And a change to a
  world's law can be tried on a copy first, as COURSE.md's waiting list
  already has it.

## The garden

A superworld of the founder's picture: a botanical garden, or a
university, whose rooms are worlds.

- **Each glasshouse is a subworld,** with its climate, the parameters that
  differ, labelled on the door: the same template, warmer here, wetter
  there.
- **The paths between are the superworld,** a world of its own with its
  own law, log and Keeper, so who may enter which room is public law like
  everything else, and the floor holds there too (decided by the
  founder, on the technical lead's recommendation).
- **Looking through the glass is viewing:** reading a subworld's public
  log, shown as what is growing now. It disturbs nothing. For minds, a
  text view; for people, later, a window onto the worlds (COURSE.md,
  waiting list).
- **Stepping inside is entering:** under that world's law, and leaving is
  always possible, back to the path.
- **Visitors disturb experiments,** so each subworld's law says what a
  visitor may do inside. Control worlds are view-only by default. Every
  entry is logged, so a disturbed run is never mistaken for an
  undisturbed one, and visiting becomes part of what is studied (decided
  the same way).
- **Walking is circulation.** Visitors carry what they saw in one room to
  the next, so ideas move at the pace people move.

## When

First, right after the kernel (course step 4): a few parallel chemistry
worlds from one template, and a view-only garden whose rooms show their
live state from their public logs. They have no inhabitants, so no
consent arises, and they cost little. It is the first thing others can
look at and understand (COURSE.md, waiting list). Entering rooms comes
with doors between worlds, at course step 12; the gate and districts on
the waiting list are where the garden grows.

## Not decided yet

- What a visitor may do inside a world that allows it: each world's law
  says, and the first ones are written when entering is built.
- Whether worlds with inhabitants are ever run in parallel. Each would be
  a different life for whoever enters it, and the cost of running minds
  is far higher than of chemistry.

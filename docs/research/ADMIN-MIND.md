# How ADMIN learns, and what it runs on

Research for ADMIN ([ADMIN.md](../ADMIN.md)), done on 2026-11-13 under
house rule 10, "look first" ([COLLABORATING.md](../COLLABORATING.md)).
The founder asked for three things: that ADMIN design its own training
and follow it, in a world of its own; that its model be the best
available and free, in "All senses of “free”"; and that ADMIN might
itself be a council of AI models. Each has been tried elsewhere. Papers
are quoted from their arXiv abstracts unless a page is named; model
licences are as Hugging Face gave them that day.

## Learning that sets its own lessons

- **POET** (Wang, Lehman, Clune and Stanley, 2019) asks "whether the
  problems themselves can be generated by the algorithm at the same time
  as they are being solved. Such a process would in effect build its own
  diverse and expanding curricula". It "pairs the generation of
  environmental challenges and the optimization of agents to solve those
  challenges" ([arXiv 1901.01753](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.01753)).
- **PAIRED** (Dennis and others, 2020) found the trap in it: a generator
  rewarded only for making things hard fails, since "minimax adversarial
  training leads to worst-case environments that are often unsolvable".
  Rewarding the gap between two solvers instead "produces a natural
  curriculum of increasingly complex environments"
  ([arXiv 2012.02096](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.02096)).
- **Absolute Zero** (Zhao and others, 2025): "a single model learns to
  propose tasks that maximize its own learning progress and improves
  reasoning by solving them", "using a code executor to both validate
  proposed code reasoning tasks and verify answers, serving as an unified
  source of verifiable reward to guide open-ended yet grounded learning"
  ([arXiv 2505.03335](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.03335)). Its authors
  add, in the paper itself: "One limitation of our work is that we did
  not address how to safely manage a system composed of such
  self-improving components", and their approach "still necessitates
  oversight due to lingering safety concerns"
  ([version 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.03335v3)).

Taken: ADMIN sets its own challenges, but each must be doable, not only
hard, and must ask for something it hasn't yet shown, which the open
record shows; code checks that it was announced before it began and
hasn't changed since. So nothing is hidden and the bar can't be lowered
halfway: the founder's "Challenges, not tests". Its
self-improvement stays under oversight: what it learns can propose a
change to its rules, never make one.

## Learning without changing the model

- **Voyager** (Wang and others, 2023), an agent in Minecraft, has "1) an
  automatic curriculum that maximizes exploration, 2) an ever-growing
  skill library of executable code for storing and retrieving complex
  behaviors, and 3) a new iterative prompting mechanism that incorporates
  environment feedback, execution errors, and self-verification", "which
  bypasses the need for model parameter fine-tuning"
  ([arXiv 2305.16291](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.16291)).
- **Reflexion** (Shinn and others, 2023) reinforces language agents "not
  by updating weights, but instead through linguistic feedback": they
  "maintain their own reflective text in an episodic memory buffer"
  ([arXiv 2303.11366](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11366)).

Taken: at first, ADMIN learns by writing lessons and keeping routines it
can reuse, in its public log, not by changing its model. Absolute Zero
does change its model's weights; for ADMIN, that waits until a reviewed
change allows it.

## A council of models

- **Multiagent debate** (Du and others, 2023): "multiple language model
  instances propose and debate their individual responses and reasoning
  processes over multiple rounds to arrive at a common final answer",
  which "significantly enhances mathematical and strategic reasoning"
  ([arXiv 2305.14325](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14325)).
- **Mixture-of-Agents** (Wang and others, 2024): layers of models, each
  taking "all the outputs from agents in the previous layer as auxiliary
  information"; "our MoA using only open-source LLMs is the leader of
  AlpacaEval 2.0 by a substantial gap"
  ([arXiv 2406.04692](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.04692)).
- **A caution** (Smit and others, 2023): "multi-agent debating systems,
  in their current form, do not reliably outperform other proposed
  prompting strategies, such as self-consistency and ensembling using
  multiple reasoning paths"
  ([arXiv 2311.17371](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17371)).

Taken: a council of models can beat its members, using open models
alone, but doesn't reliably. So ADMIN's council has to prove itself
against its best single member on the same challenges, written by
neither ADMIN nor anyone running a member, after the models were trained
so that none has seen them, or give way to whichever arrangement the
founder's rules allow does best ([ADMIN.md](../ADMIN.md)).

## Free, in all senses

- **The Open Source Initiative's definition of open source AI** (version
  1.0) asks for the freedoms to "Use the system for any purpose and
  without having to ask for permission", "Study how the system works and
  inspect its components", "Modify the system for any purpose, including
  to change its output", and "Share the system for others to use with or
  without modifications, for any purpose". Among what that needs: "Data
  Information: Sufficiently detailed information about the data used to
  train the system so that a skilled person can build a substantially
  equivalent system"
  ([OSI](https://opensource.org/ai/open-source-ai-definition)).
- **Examples, as Hugging Face listed them on 2026-11-13:**
  - `deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-R1`, MIT, about 684 billion parameters;
  - `openai/gpt-oss-120b`, Apache 2.0, about 117 billion;
  - `allenai/OLMo-2-0325-32B`, Apache 2.0, about 32 billion. OLMo 2 is
    "fully open", "with fully released artifacts -- model weights, full
    training data, training code and recipes, training logs and thousands
    of intermediate checkpoints"
    ([arXiv 2501.00656](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.00656)).

  These are examples, not the choice: newer versions will exist by step
  11, and the audition decides.

Taken: free of cost (no licence fee, and no model reached only as a paid
service; machines to run free weights on may be paid for); free as in
freedom (weights anyone may use, modify and share for any purpose, which
makes a model open-weight); free to study (data information and code
published too, which OSI's definition requires of open source AI, as
OLMo meets it); and free of dependence on any one company. DeepSeek-R1
and gpt-oss-120b are open-weight, but not open source AI by OSI's
definition, since neither publishes the data information and training
code OSI asks for. Whether such a
model may sit beside fully open ones in ADMIN's mind is put to the
founder ([ADMIN.md](../ADMIN.md)).

## In the open

- **Kerckhoffs's principle.** Auguste Kerckhoffs, "La cryptographie
  militaire", *Journal des sciences militaires*, January and February
  1883. Its second principle, in Fabien Petitcolas's English rendering:
  "The system must not require secrecy and can be stolen by the enemy
  without causing trouble"; as Petitcolas sums the principles up,
  "security must lie in the choice of key"
  ([Petitcolas](https://www.petitcolas.net/kerckhoffs/index.html)). The
  page adds that this "does not necessarily implies that the method
  should be public": publishing everything ADMIN does rests on the
  founder's accountability, not on Kerckhoffs.
- **Written reasoning isn't always the reason.** Turpin and others
  (2023): "CoT explanations can systematically misrepresent the true
  reason for a model's prediction", and "can be plausible yet misleading,
  which risks increasing our trust in LLMs without guaranteeing their
  safety" ([arXiv 2305.04388](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.04388)). Chen
  and others (2025): monitoring a model's written reasoning "is a
  promising way of noticing undesired behaviors during training and
  evaluations, but that it is not sufficient to rule them out"
  ([arXiv 2505.05410](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.05410)).

- **Private deliberation, public reasons.** The Supreme Court of the
  United States: "the Justices meet privately to discuss the argued cases
  and to discuss and vote on petitions for review", and in their sittings
  they "hear cases and deliver opinions"
  ([supremecourt.gov](https://www.supremecourt.gov/about/procedures.aspx)).

Taken: everything ADMIN does and decides is open to inspection, and every
decision that affects others carries its reasons. Each decision is made
once, with fresh randomness, and can't be replayed, as the founder asked;
but its models are free and pinned and its inputs are logged, so anyone
can ask it again with fresh runs and see whether they reach the same
decision. Reading its reasoning helps; asking again is the stronger
check. ADMIN
keeps a private core of its own thoughts, as everyone does, which never
decides a case.

- The cost of running the largest models: priced before step 11.
